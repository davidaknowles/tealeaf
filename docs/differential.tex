\section*{Overview}

\subsection*{Questions and unit of analysis}

The analysis asks two distinct questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Does local splice-path usage differ by experimental condition within
        a cell type?
  \item Does local splice-path usage differ across cell types within the same
        mice?
\end{enumerate}
The primary analysis aggregates cells into
\emph{mouse-by-condition-by-cell-type pseudobulks}. An evaluated alternative
retains each cell's EC counts and transcript baseline in the local likelihood
but estimates one shared path effect for the pseudobulk. Both approaches keep
biological replication at the mouse level; cells are never treated as
independent condition replicates.

A splice block is a local region between constitutive exonic anchors.
Transcripts that take the same path through that region are collapsed to one
subisoform. This targets local splicing choices, which can be identifiable
even when the corresponding full-length transcripts are not.

For condition effects, each hypothesis is indexed by a splice block and a
cell type. It is an omnibus test of
\[
  H_0:\quad\text{mean path usage is equal across the included conditions}.
\]
Only conditions represented by at least three mouse pseudobulks are included.
With two conditions and two paths, this is a precision-weighted
differential-PSI test. With more conditions or paths, all condition
coefficients and path log-ratios are tested jointly.

For cell-type effects, each hypothesis is indexed only by a nonredundant
splice-path partition. It is an omnibus test of equal path usage across the
included cell types, after absorbing mouse and condition into a
mouse-specific fixed effect. Cell types must be represented in at least
three mice, and the primary FDR family requires at least 30 pseudobulk
observations per partition. These two hypothesis families are reported
separately.

\subsection*{Workflow}

The analysis proceeds in five steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Construct local splice blocks from the transcript annotation and
        collapse transcripts to paths within each block.
  \item Aggregate paired oligo(dT) and random-hexamer EC counts, and
        hierarchical transcript estimates, within each mouse, condition, and
        cell type.
  \item Refine path usage in each pseudobulk with the exact paired-primer EC
        likelihood. Optionally, retain separate cell likelihood terms while
        sharing one pseudobulk path perturbation.
  \item Estimate path-logratio covariance from EC ambiguity and UMI sampling.
        Estimates with weak information or boundary path usage are retained
        in output but excluded from Gaussian testing.
  \item Fit mouse-replicate GLS condition models and compositional cell-type
        models. Test condition within cell type and cell type within mouse as
        separate hypothesis families.
\end{enumerate}

Two inferential covariance calculations are reported. The
\emph{conditional} calculation fixes the fitted transcript mixture within
each path. The \emph{profile} calculation allows all transcript directions
to vary as nuisance parameters. They are sensitivity analyses for the same
block-by-cell-type hypothesis, not independent biological hypotheses.

\section*{Notation and Dimensions}

An \emph{equivalence class} (EC) is a set of transcripts compatible with one
UMI. An EC count is therefore observed, whereas its allocation among
compatible transcripts is latent. A \emph{splice path} is an ordered local
exon chain between two constitutive anchors. A \emph{subisoform} is the set
of full-length transcripts taking the same path through one block. PSI means
percent spliced in; for two paths it is one component of the path-composition
vector. ILR means isometric log-ratio, an orthonormal coordinate system for a
composition.

For block \(b\), let \(n_b,T_b,S_b\in\mathbb N\) be its numbers of
qualifying pseudobulks, modeled transcript features, and spliced paths,
respectively, with \(S_b\geq2\). Then \(d_b=S_b-1\in\mathbb N\) is its
number of independent log-ratios. There are \(P=2\) primer protocols.
Primer \(p\) has \(E_{bp}\in\mathbb N\) ECs for block \(b\). Pseudobulk
\(i\) contains \(H_{bi}\in\mathbb N\) cells. The principal symbols are:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\toprule
\text{symbol} & \text{dimension or domain} & \text{meaning}\\
\midrule
b & \{1,\ldots,\mathcal B\} & \text{splice-block index}\\
i & \{1,\ldots,n_b\} & \text{pseudobulk index within block }b\\
p & \{1,\ldots,P\} & \text{primer-protocol index}\\
h & \{1,\ldots,H_{bi}\} & \text{cell index within pseudobulk }i\\
t & \{1,\ldots,T_b\} & \text{transcript-feature index}\\
s & \{1,\ldots,S_b\} & \text{splice-path index}\\
\bm c_{bip} & \mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}} & \text{pseudobulk EC counts}\\
A_{bp} & \mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}\times T_b} & \text{weighted EC design}\\
\bm\theta_{bi} & \mathbb R_+^{T_b} & \text{transcript abundance}\\
C_b & \{0,1\}^{S_b\times T_b} & \text{transcript-to-path map}\\
Q_b & \mathbb R^{S_b\times d_b} & \text{Helmert ILR basis}\\
\bm\delta_{bi} & \mathbb R^{d_b} & \text{local path perturbation}\\
\bm\psi_{bi} & \Delta^{S_b-1} & \text{path composition}\\
\bm y_{bi} & \mathbb R^{d_b} & \text{ILR response}\\
V_{bi} & \mathbb S_+^{d_b} & \text{ILR inferential covariance}\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Here \(\mathcal B\in\mathbb N\) is the number of blocks,
\(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,\ldots\}\),
\(\Delta^{S_b-1}=\{\bm v\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b}:\bm1^\prime\bm v=1\}\),
and \(\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) is the set of symmetric positive-semidefinite
\(d_b\times d_b\) matrices. Bold lower-case symbols are vectors, upper-case
symbols are matrices, \(\bm1_k\in\mathbb R^k\) is a vector of ones,
\(\bm0_k\in\mathbb R^k\) is a vector of zeros,
\(I_k\in\mathbb R^{k\times k}\) is the identity, \(\odot\) is elementwise
multiplication, and \((\cdot)^+\) is the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse.

\subsection*{Helmert basis and ILR coordinates}

The simplex has only \(d_b=S_b-1\) free directions because path proportions
sum to one. The Helmert matrix \(Q_b\) is a fixed orthonormal basis for the
sum-zero subspace:
\[
 Q_b^\prime Q_b=I_{d_b},\qquad Q_b^\prime\bm1_{S_b}=\bm0_{d_b}.
\]
Using one-based row \(r\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\) and column
\(j\in\{1,\ldots,d_b\}\), its entries are
\[
 (Q_b)_{rj}=
 \begin{cases}
  1/\sqrt{j(j+1)}, & r\leq j,\\
  -j/\sqrt{j(j+1)}, & r=j+1,\\
  0, & r>j+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
For any strictly positive \(\bm\psi_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\), the ILR
coordinate is \(\bm y_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\),
where the logarithm is elementwise. For \(S_b=2\),
\(Q_b=(1,-1)^\prime/\sqrt2\) and
\(y_{bi}=\log(\psi_{bi1}/\psi_{bi2})/\sqrt2\). Thus a two-path ILR test is a
scaled log-odds, or differential-PSI, test.

\section*{Statistical Model}

\subsection*{Paired-primer EC likelihood}

For block \(b\), pseudobulk \(i\), and primer \(p\), define
\(N_{bip}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm c_{bip}\in\mathbb N_0\),
\(\bm q_{bip}=A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}}\), and
\(Z_{bip}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm q_{bip}\in\mathbb R_+\). Conditional on
the primer-specific UMI total,
\[
 \bm c_{bip}\mid N_{bip},\bm\theta_{bi}
 \sim\operatorname{Multinomial}
 \left(N_{bip},\bm q_{bip}/Z_{bip}\right).
\]
The oligo(dT) and random-hexamer protocols use separate \(A_{bp}\) matrices
and likelihood terms. Their different transcript exposures therefore model
primer-specific positional coverage without forcing a common bias profile.

The genome-wide hierarchical model supplies
\(\bm\theta_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_b}\). Let
\(s_b(t)\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\) map a spliced transcript feature \(t\) to its
path, and let \(\mathcal T_b^{\rm sp}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,T_b\}\) be the
spliced features. Their baseline mass is the scalar
\(\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_b^{\rm sp}}
\theta_{bit}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+\). For
\(t\in\mathcal T_b^{\rm sp}\), local refinement uses
\[
 \theta_{bit}(\bm\delta_{bi})
 =\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}\frac{\theta_{bit}^{(0)}
 \exp\{(Q_b)_{s_b(t),\cdot}\bm\delta_{bi}\}}
 {\sum_{u\in\mathcal T_b^{\rm sp}}\theta_{biu}^{(0)}
 \exp\{(Q_b)_{s_b(u),\cdot}\bm\delta_{bi}\}},
\]
where \((Q_b)_{s,\cdot}\in\mathbb R^{1\times d_b}\). Nuisance-feature
abundances are unchanged. This preserves total spliced mass and the relative
transcript mixture within each path while estimating only
\(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\).

\subsection*{Path response and inferential covariance}

Each spliced column of \(C_b\in\{0,1\}^{S_b\times T_b}\) contains one 1 in
row \(s_b(t)\); nuisance columns are zero. Define
\[
 \bm a_{bi}=C_b\bm\theta_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b},\qquad
 \bm\psi_{bi}=\frac{\bm a_{bi}}
 {\bm1_{S_b}^\prime\bm a_{bi}}\in\Delta^{S_b-1},\qquad
 \bm y_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}.
\]
The perturbation parameterization implies
\(\bm y_{bi}=\bm y_{bi}^{(0)}+\bm\delta_{bi}\), where
\(\bm y_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) is computed from the baseline.

Let \(I_{\delta,bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) be the expected Fisher information
of the summed paired-primer log likelihood with respect to
\(\bm\delta_{bi}\). The conditional covariance is
\(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}=I_{\delta,bi}^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) when the
information is positive definite.

The profile calculation instead allows all transcript directions to vary.
Set \(\bm\eta_{bi}=\log\bm\theta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{T_b}\),
\(D_{bi}=\operatorname{diag}(\bm\theta_{bi})\in
\mathbb R^{T_b\times T_b}\),
\(\bm d_{bp}=A_{bp}^\prime\bm1_{E_{bp}}\in\mathbb R^{T_b}\), and
\(\bm r_{0,bip}=(\bm d_{bp}\odot\bm\theta_{bi})/Z_{bip}
\in\mathbb R^{T_b}\).
The primer-specific expected information is
\[
 I_{\eta,bip}=N_{bip}\left[
 \frac{D_{bi}A_{bp}^\prime
 \operatorname{diag}(\bm q_{bip}^{-1})A_{bp}D_{bi}}{Z_{bip}}
 -\bm r_{0,bip}\bm r_{0,bip}^\prime
 \right]\in\mathbb S_+^{T_b}.
\]
Let \(J_{bi}=\partial\bm y_{bi}/\partial\bm\eta_{bi}^\prime
\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times T_b}\). Then
\[
 V_{bi}^{\rm prof}=J_{bi}
 \left(\sum_{p=1}^P I_{\eta,bip}\right)^+J_{bi}^\prime
 \in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
If a row direction of \(J_{bi}\) overlaps the information null space, that
contrast is marked unidentified rather than assigned zero variance by the
pseudoinverse.

\subsection*{Cell-resolved likelihood}

For cell \(h\) in pseudobulk \(i\), let
\(\bm c_{bihp}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}}\),
\(N_{bihp}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm c_{bihp}\in\mathbb N_0\), and
\(\bm\theta_{bih}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_b}\) be its EC counts, total, and
baseline. Every cell shares the same pseudobulk perturbation
\(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), but its transcript vector
\(\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})\in\mathbb R_+^{T_b}\) is normalized using
its own baseline. The objective is the scalar
\[
 \ell_{bi}(\bm\delta_{bi})=
 \sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}\sum_{p=1}^P
 \log\operatorname{Multinomial}\left(
 \bm c_{bihp};N_{bihp},
 \frac{A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})}
 {\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})}
 \right).
\]
Thus cells contribute assignment information but do not become biological
replicates. Let \(U_{bih}\in\mathbb R_+\) be the cell's total UMI count and
\(g_{bih}\in[0,1]\) its decoded proportion for the block's gene. The scalar
weight is \(w_{bih}=U_{bih}g_{bih}\), and the reported pseudobulk composition
is
\[
 \bm\psi_{bi}=
 \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}w_{bih}\bm\psi_{bih}}
 {\sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}w_{bih}}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}.
\]
Cell- and primer-specific Fisher information matrices in
\(\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) are summed, inverted, and propagated through this
weighted average to obtain \(V_{bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\).

\subsection*{Effective counts and compositional regression}

Treating all gene UMIs as known path assignments is overconfident when ECs
are ambiguous. For each fitted \(\bm\psi_{bi}\), define the unit-multinomial
ILR covariance
\[
 G_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\operatorname{diag}(\bm\psi_{bi}^{-1})Q_b
 \in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
The scalar effective count matches \(G_{bi}/n\) to the EC-derived
\(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\) in Frobenius inner product
\(\langle U,W\rangle_F=\operatorname{tr}(U^\prime W)\), for
\(U,W\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times d_b}\):
\[
 n_{bi}^{\rm eff}=
 \min\left\{N_{bi}^{\rm gene},
 \frac{\langle G_{bi},G_{bi}\rangle_F}
 {\langle G_{bi},V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\rangle_F}\right\}\in\mathbb R_+,
\]
where \(N_{bi}^{\rm gene}\in\mathbb N_0\) is the gene UMI count. Fractional
path counts are
\(\bm x_{bi}=n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b}\), with
\(\bm1_{S_b}^\prime\bm x_{bi}=n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\).

For one block, stack \(n=n_b\in\mathbb N\) observations in a design
\(X\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\), where \(k\in\mathbb N\), and coefficient matrix
\(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\). The mean composition
\(\bm\mu_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\) is defined by reference-path logits
\[
 \log\frac{\mu_{bis}}{\mu_{biS_b}}
 =(X\Beta_b)_{is},\qquad s=1,\ldots,d_b.
\]
The generalized Dirichlet--multinomial likelihood, extended to fractional
counts through gamma functions, is
\[
 \bm x_{bi}\mid n_{bi}^{\rm eff}
 \sim\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}
 \left(n_{bi}^{\rm eff},\kappa_b\bm\mu_{bi}\right),
\]
where \(\kappa_b\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) is a block-level
concentration. Larger \(\kappa_b\) means less overdispersion; the endpoint
\(\kappa_b=\infty\) is exactly multinomial.

For nested designs \(X_0\in\mathbb R^{n\times k_0}\) and
\(X_1\in\mathbb R^{n\times k_1}\), with \(k_1\geq k_0\) and the first
\(k_0,k_1\in\mathbb N\), and the first \(k_0\) columns of \(X_1\) equal to
\(X_0\), let
\(\widehat\ell_0,\widehat\ell_1\in\mathbb R\) be maximized scalar log
likelihoods. The statistic and nominal degrees of freedom are
\[
 \Lambda_b=2(\widehat\ell_1-\widehat\ell_0)\in\mathbb R_+,
 \qquad \nu_b=(k_1-k_0)d_b\in\mathbb N_0.
\]
The fixed-concentration analysis estimates
\(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) under \(X_0\) and uses
it under \(X_1\). The free-concentration sensitivity estimates
\(\widehat\kappa_{1b}\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) under \(X_1\). In the latter case both
models contain one concentration nuisance parameter, so \(\nu_b\) is
unchanged. Both finite and exact multinomial endpoints are evaluated.

\subsection*{Gaussian mouse-replicate regression}

For a condition test within one block and cell type, stack
\(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) over \(n\in\mathbb N\) mice.
Let \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\), \(k\in\mathbb N\), contain an intercept and condition
indicators, \(\Beta_b^{\rm GLS}\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\) be regression
coefficients, and \(\bm\epsilon_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) be residuals:
\[
 \widehat{\bm y}_{bi}=(X_{b,i\cdot}\Beta_b^{\rm GLS})^\prime
 +\bm\epsilon_{bi},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\bm\epsilon_{bi})=
 V_{bi}+\tau_b^2I_{d_b}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
Here \(X_{b,i\cdot}\in\mathbb R^{1\times k}\), and
\(\tau_b^2\in\mathbb R_+\) is residual between-mouse variance estimated by
REML under the null. If \(r_b\in\mathbb N\) non-reference condition columns are
tested, their coefficients form
\(\bm\beta_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb R^{r_bd_b}\) with covariance
\(\Sigma_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb S_+^{r_bd_b}\). The Wald statistic
is
\[
 W_b=(\bm\beta_b^{\rm test})^\prime
 (\Sigma_b^{\rm test})^{-1}\bm\beta_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb R_+,
 \qquad \nu_b^{\rm GLS}=r_bd_b.
\]

\subsection*{Paired cell-type test and permutations}

For a cell-type test, let \(M_b,L_b\in\mathbb N\) be the numbers of
represented biological mice and cell types, respectively. The null design
\(X_{0b}\in\{0,1\}^{n_b\times M_b}\) contains an intercept-coded set of
mouse fixed effects. The alternative
\(X_{1b}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) appends treatment-coded
cell-type indicators. Consequently
\(k_0=M_b\), \(k_1=M_b+L_b-1\), and
\(\nu_b=(L_b-1)d_b\). The tested coefficient block is
\(\Beta_b^{\rm cell}\in\mathbb R^{(L_b-1)\times d_b}\), and
\[
 H_{0b}:\Beta_b^{\rm cell}=0
 \quad\text{versus}\quad
 H_{1b}:\Beta_b^{\rm cell}\neq0.
\]
Mouse effects absorb condition and every shift shared by cell types from the
same mouse.

For \(R=500\in\mathbb N\) permutations and
\(r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}\), cell-type labels are shuffled only
among rows from the same mouse, producing
\(X_{1b}^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) and statistic
\(\Lambda_b^{(r)}\in\mathbb R_+\). Let
\(\varepsilon_b=10^{-6}\max(1,|\Lambda_b|)\in\mathbb R_+\).
The finite-permutation p-value is
\[
 p_b=\frac{1+\sum_{r=1}^{R}
 \mathbb{1}\{\Lambda_b^{(r)}\geq\Lambda_b-\varepsilon_b\}}{R+1}\in(0,1],
 \qquad R=500.
\]
The null coefficients and \(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\) are reused, but each
permuted alternative is optimized. The tolerance counts numerically equal
likelihood optima as ties. Benjamini--Hochberg correction is applied across
canonical partitions with at least 30 pseudobulk rows.

\subsection*{Joint condition tests}

ACAT combines \(J\in\mathbb N\) component p-values
\(p_j\in(0,1)\), \(j=1,\ldots,J\), into the scalar
\[
 p_{\rm ACAT}=\frac12-\frac1\pi\arctan\left[
 \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\tan\{\pi(1/2-p_j)\}\right]\in(0,1).
\]
One level combines cell-type-specific p-values for a path partition; a
second combines distinct partitions within a gene.

The shared-effect Gaussian model for block \(b\) uses
\(M\in\mathbb N\) mice indexed by \(m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), and
\(L_m\in\mathbb N\) cell types for mouse \(m\), indexed by
\(c\in\{1,\ldots,L_m\}\). Its response is
\(\bm y_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), design row
\(X_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{1\times k}\), where \(k\in\mathbb N\), coefficient matrix
\(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\), mouse random effect
\(\bm u_{bm}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), and residual
\(\bm e_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\):
\[
 \bm y_{bmc}=(X_{bmc}\Beta_b)^\prime+\bm u_{bm}+\bm e_{bmc}.
\]
The random terms satisfy
\(\operatorname E(\bm u_{bm})=\operatorname E(\bm e_{bmc})
=\bm0_{d_b}\),
\(\operatorname{Var}(\bm u_{bm})=\tau_{\rm mouse}^2I_{d_b}\), and
\(\operatorname{Var}(\bm e_{bmc})=
V_{bmc}+\tau_{\rm resid}^2I_{d_b}\).
They are independent across mice, and residuals are independent across cell
types conditional on \(\bm u_{bm}\). Hence, for \(c\neq c'\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\bm y_{bmc},\bm y_{bmc'})=
 \tau_{\rm mouse}^2I_{d_b},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\bm y_{bmc})=
 V_{bmc}+(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2+\tau_{\rm resid}^2)I_{d_b},
\]
where \(V_{bmc}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\), and
\(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2,\tau_{\rm resid}^2\in\mathbb R_+\) are shared-mouse
and residual variance
components estimated by two-component REML under the no-condition model.
Condition labels are permuted once per mouse and applied to all its cell
types.

\section*{Algorithms}

All algorithms operate independently over canonical blocks unless a joint
gene-level combination is stated. ``Fit'' below means bounded L-BFGS with
analytic gradients and a fourfold iteration-limit retry after a warm start
from the first endpoint.

\subsection*{Algorithm 1: construct splice blocks and path maps}

\textbf{Input:} for one gene, \(T_g\in\mathbb N\) annotated transcripts and
their ordered zero-based half-open exon intervals.  \textbf{Output:} blocks
\(b\), maps \(C_b\in\{0,1\}^{S_b\times T_b}\), and bases
\(Q_b\in\mathbb R^{S_b\times(S_b-1)}\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Split the gene at every annotated exon boundary to obtain atomic
        genomic intervals.
  \item Mark intervals exonic in every spliced transcript; merge adjacent
        marked intervals into constitutive anchors.
  \item For each consecutive anchor pair, record each transcript's ordered
        chain of intervening exonic intervals. Use terminal pseudo-anchors
        when no shared first or last anchor exists.
  \item Assign identical chains to the same path. Discard a block if
        \(S_b=1\); otherwise construct one-hot transcript columns in \(C_b\).
  \item Construct \(Q_b\) from the Helmert formula above.
  \item Canonicalize the unordered collection of transcript sets assigned to
        paths. Retain one representative for identical partitions and attach
        all equivalent interval identifiers to it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 2: pseudobulk local fit and covariance}

\textbf{Input:} \(\bm c_{bip}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}}\),
\(A_{bp}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}\times T_b}\), baseline
\(\bm\theta_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_b}\), \(C_b\), and \(Q_b\).
\textbf{Output:} \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\),
\(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), and conditional/profile
covariances in \(\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Initialize \(\bm\delta_{bi}\in[-20,20]^{d_b}\) at zero; for
        \(S_b=2\), also evaluate a fixed log-ratio grid and retain its best
        point.
  \item Form \(\bm\theta_{bi}(\bm\delta_{bi})\) while preserving spliced
        mass and within-path transcript mixtures.
  \item Sum the two primer-specific multinomial log likelihoods and optimize
        over \(\bm\delta_{bi}\).
  \item Map the fitted transcript vector through \(C_b\), normalize to
        \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\), and compute
        \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log
        \widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\).
  \item Sum primer Fisher information for \(\bm\delta_{bi}\) and invert it
        to obtain \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\). Mark the response unidentified if
        the information is singular in a tested path direction.
  \item For the profile sensitivity, sum
        \(I_{\eta,bip}\in\mathbb S_+^{T_b}\), compute
        \(J_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times T_b}\), and form
        \(V_{bi}^{\rm prof}\). Apply the information-null-space check.
  \item Write every estimate and its convergence, identifiability, and
        reliability flags; do not silently replace a failed covariance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 3: cell-resolved local fit}

\textbf{Input:} cell EC vectors
\(\bm c_{bihp}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}}\), cell baselines
\(\bm\theta_{bih}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_b}\), weights
\(w_{bih}\in\mathbb R_+\), and the same \(A_{bp},C_b,Q_b\).
\textbf{Output:} one pseudobulk response and covariance with dimensions
identical to Algorithm 2.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Allocate one shared \(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\); do not
        allocate a free effect per cell.
  \item For every cell and primer, perturb its own baseline, normalize its EC
        probabilities, and add its multinomial log likelihood.
  \item Optimize the summed objective over the shared \(\bm\delta_{bi}\).
  \item Compute each fitted cell composition
        \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bih}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\) and take the normalized
        \(w_{bih}\)-weighted average.
  \item Sum the \(d_b\)-by-\(d_b\) cell-by-primer Fisher matrices, invert,
        and propagate through the weighted composition average.
  \item Send the resulting pseudobulk response to exactly the same
        mouse-replicate test as Algorithm 4.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 4: Gaussian condition and cell-type tests}

\textbf{Input:} \(n\) responses
\(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), covariances
\(V_{bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\), and metadata for their mice, conditions,
and cell types. \textbf{Output:} one Wald p-value for a condition-within-cell-
type hypothesis or one paired cell-type hypothesis.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item For a condition test, retain conditions with at least three
        biological mice. Construct
        \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\) from an intercept and condition
        indicators, and form its reduced design
        \(X_{0b}\in\mathbb R^{n\times(k-r_b)}\) by deleting the
        \(r_b\in\mathbb N\) tested condition columns.
  \item For a cell-type test, let the \(n=n_b\) rows represent \(M_b\) mice
        and \(L_b\) cell types. Construct
        \(X_{0b}\in\{0,1\}^{n_b\times M_b}\) from mouse effects and
        \(X_{1b}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) by appending
        \(L_b-1\) cell-type indicators. Require the applicable full and
        reduced designs to have full column rank.
  \item Under the reduced model, estimate
        \(\tau_b^2\in\mathbb R_+\) by REML using covariance
        \(V_{bi}+\tau_b^2I_{d_b}\).
  \item Hold \(\widehat\tau_b^2\) fixed and fit
        the applicable full design by GLS. Its coefficient matrix is
        \(k\times d_b\) for the condition model and
        \((M_b+L_b-1)\times d_b\) for the cell-type model.
  \item Stack the tested coefficients: \(r_bd_b\) entries for condition or
        \((L_b-1)d_b\) for cell type. Compute \(W_b\) and compare it with
        the corresponding chi-squared reference distribution.
  \item For calibration, permute condition once per mouse for a condition
        test, or shuffle cell-type labels only within mouse for a cell-type
        test. Hold \(\widehat\tau_b^2\) fixed and refit each alternative.
  \item Apply BH separately by biological question and by conditional versus
        profile covariance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 5: paired compositional cell-type test}

\textbf{Input:} path estimates and conditional covariances from Algorithm 2.
\textbf{Output:} one exact permutation p-value per canonical block.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Keep converged, conditionally identifiable estimates. Require each
        modeled path proportion to be at least 0.05 and each represented cell
        type to occur in at least three mice.
  \item Convert each \((\bm\psi_{bi},V_{bi}^{\rm cond})\) pair to
        \(n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\in\mathbb R_+\) and
        \(\bm x_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b}\).
  \item Build \(X_{0b}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times M_b}\) from mouse fixed
        effects and \(X_{1b}\in
        \mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) by appending cell-type effects.
  \item Fit the null coefficients and \(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\). Fit the
        observed alternative and compute \(\Lambda_b\).
  \item For the fixed-\(\kappa\) approach, set
        \(\kappa_{1b}=\widehat\kappa_{0b}\). For the free-\(\kappa\)
        sensitivity, optimize \(\widehat\kappa_{1b}\) and compare the best finite
        fit with the exact multinomial endpoint.
  \item Repeat \(R=500\) times: shuffle cell-type labels within each mouse,
        rebuild only the appended columns of \(X_{1b}^{(r)}\), reuse the null
        fit, refit the alternative, and store \(\Lambda_b^{(r)}\).
  \item Compute the tolerance-aware finite-permutation rank \(p_b\). Require
        \(n_b\geq30\), then apply BH across canonical blocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 6: pairwise and dominant-path sensitivities}

\textbf{Input:} the same path estimates as Algorithm 5. \textbf{Output:}
calibrated sensitivity tables, not additional primary discoveries.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Pairwise:} for each cell-type pair, retain only mice containing
        both types. Use \(X_0\in\mathbb R^{2M\times M}\) for \(M\) mouse
        effects and \(X_1\in\mathbb R^{2M\times(M+1)}\) for one cell-type
        effect. Permute the two labels within mouse and correct across every
        block-by-pair hypothesis.
  \item \emph{Dominant paths:} rank paths by the scalar aggregate
        \(\sum_iN_{bi}^{\rm gene}\psi_{bis}\). Select index set
        \(\mathcal S_b\subseteq\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\), with
        \(|\mathcal S_b|=2\), without using labels.
  \item Let \(E_b\in\{0,1\}^{S_b\times2}\) select these paths and let
        \(Q_b^{(2)}\in\mathbb R^{2\times1}\) be their Helmert basis. Transform
        covariance with
        \(\mathcal R_b=(Q_b^{(2)})^\prime E_b^\prime Q_b\in
        \mathbb R^{1\times d_b}\) and
        \(V_{bi}^{(2)}=\mathcal R_bV_{bi}\mathcal R_b^\prime
        \in\mathbb R^{1\times1}\).
  \item Renormalize the two retained proportions, then run Algorithm 5.
        Report path-floor sweeps as sensitivity analyses.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Algorithm 7: joint condition tests}

\textbf{Input:} calibrated block-by-cell-type p-values or ILR responses.
\textbf{Output:} partition- or gene-level joint p-values.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{ACAT across cell types:} collect the \(J\) valid p-values for
        one canonical partition and evaluate \(p_{\rm ACAT}\). Optionally
        apply the same formula to distinct partition p-values within a gene.
  \item \emph{Naive shared effect, negative control:} fit cell-type
        intercepts and one condition coefficient while treating rows as
        independent. Retain this only to measure pseudoreplication inflation.
  \item \emph{Clustered shared GLS:} construct the block covariance whose
        diagonal blocks are
        \(V_{bmc}+(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2+\tau_{\rm resid}^2)I_{d_b}\) and whose
        same-mouse off-diagonal blocks are \(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2I_{d_b}\).
        Estimate the
        two scalar variances under the null by REML.
  \item Fit the shared condition coefficient by GLS. Permute condition once
        per mouse, synchronously across all cell types, and refit.
  \item Apply BH to the resulting canonical-partition family; do not combine
        discoveries from negative-control and calibrated methods.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Implementation Details}

\subsection*{Splice-block construction}

GTF exons are represented by zero-based, half-open intervals. Exon boundaries
define atomic intervals; intervals exonic in every annotated spliced
transcript are merged into constitutive anchors. Consecutive anchors define a
block, and a transcript path is its ordered exon-interval chain between them.
Terminal pseudo-anchors include alternative first and last regions. A gene
without a shared constitutive region receives one whole-gene block.

Blocks with one path are discarded. Simple two-path blocks include skipped
exons, mutually exclusive exons, alternative donors and acceptors, and
retained introns. The implementation also supports nested and multi-path
blocks. Precursor features remain nuisance features in the EC model but are
not assigned to spliced paths.

Different genomic blocks can induce exactly the same partition of annotated
transcripts, for example when several exons always occur together. Such
blocks have identical statistical responses and must not count as repeated
evidence. A canonical partition is the unordered collection of transcript
sets assigned to paths. Equivalent blocks are collapsed before FDR or joint
testing; all member interval identifiers remain in the output.

\subsection*{Pseudobulks and local fitting}

Cell transcript proportions from the hierarchical decoder are weighted by
cell UMI totals and aggregated by mouse, condition, and cell type.
Pseudobulks must contain at least 20 cells and \(10^5\) total UMIs. A
block is fit when its gene has at least 25 gene-assigned UMIs in at least two
qualifying pseudobulks. Only ECs whose compatible transcripts map uniquely to
that gene enter the local likelihood.

Bounded L-BFGS optimizes the paired multinomial likelihood, with
\(\|\bm\delta_{bi}\|_\infty\leq20\) to prevent floating-point underflow,
where \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) is the maximum absolute component. Two-path
fits are initialized from a log-ratio grid because the paired-primer
normalizers can make the objective non-concave.

For the cell-resolved variant, sparse cell-by-EC matrices are retained and
one local transcript matrix is decoded at a time. The optimizer dimension is
still only the number of paths minus one. Cell-specific Fisher contributions
are accumulated analytically, so the method does not fit one free splicing
parameter per cell.

\subsection*{Identifiability and reliability}

Finite Fisher covariance is not sufficient near a simplex boundary.
Parametric bootstrap identified two reliability requirements:
\[
  \lambda_{\max}(V_{bi})\leq 1/8
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \min_s\widehat\psi_{bis}\geq 0.15.
\]
Thus every tested log-ratio direction has effective information of at least
eight and every fitted path has at least 15\% usage. All estimates are
written, together with convergence, identifiability, and reliability flags;
only reliable estimates enter GLS.
Here \(\lambda_{\max}(V_{bi})\in\mathbb R_+\) is the largest eigenvalue of
the \(d_b\times d_b\) covariance, and
\(\widehat\psi_{bis}\in[0,1]\) is path \(s\)'s fitted proportion.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Condition-test scope}

The primary analysis used the 25-gene-UMI cutoff and blocks with at most
eight represented paths. It fit 10,100 blocks and 359,713
pseudobulk-by-block path estimates; 359,711 fits converged. After reliability
filtering, there were 829 conditional and 506 profile tests.

These 1,335 rows represent 829 distinct block-by-cell-type hypotheses,
covering 527 distinct splice blocks in eight cell types. The profile analysis
is available for a subset of the conditional hypotheses. The possible
conditions were 5xFAD, 5xFIRE, 5xFIRE PBS, 5xFIRE transplant, FIRE, and WT.
For the 829 conditional hypotheses, 275 compared two conditions, 155 compared
three, 153 compared four, 130 compared five, and 116 compared all six. The
included set varies because every condition in a test must have at least
three independent mouse pseudobulks for that block and cell type.

\subsection*{Condition-test results}

No block was significant at 5\% FDR in either covariance analysis. At the
unadjusted \(p<0.05\) level:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the conditional analysis identified 24 block-by-cell-type hypotheses,
        covering 21 distinct blocks;
  \item the profile analysis identified 20 block-by-cell-type hypotheses,
        covering 17 distinct blocks;
  \item 20 hypotheses were nominally significant in both analyses.
\end{itemize}
The union is 24 nominal block-by-cell-type hypotheses covering 21 blocks, but
the corrected number of significant hypotheses and blocks is zero. Thus
there is no FDR-significant evidence for condition-dependent splicing within
cell type. The two covariance variants should not be added as if they were
separate discoveries.

\subsection*{Cell-type effects within mouse}

The initial Gaussian analysis tested 369 conditional and 218 profile
partitions. It found 22 and five FDR-significant partitions, respectively,
with all profile discoveries contained in the conditional set. After
canonicalizing annotation intervals that encode the same
transcript-to-path partition, these 22 intervals represented 14 distinct
partitions.

The compositional analysis used converged, conditionally identifiable
estimates and required every modeled path proportion to be at least 5\%.
This produced 646 nonredundant tests. A minimum of 30 pseudobulk observations
per partition, chosen as a label-effect-independent coverage requirement,
retained 406 hypotheses for FDR correction. Among these, 149 had exact
permutation \(p<0.05\), and
\textbf{101 splice-path partitions in 95 genes passed 5\% FDR}. Thirteen of
the 14 nonredundant Gaussian discoveries were recovered; the remaining
partition had exact \(p=0.032\) but FDR 0.098. Significant compositional
results had median maximum cell-type logit effect 0.84 and median effective
path count 21.7.

The observation-count threshold is not a narrow boundary effect: thresholds
from 29 through 32 pseudobulks each gave 101 discoveries, while 33 gave 99.
All 646 observed fits converged. The median number of valid permutations was
500; the minimum was 476 after excluding failed permutation refits.
Asymptotic Dirichlet--multinomial p-values were anti-conservative, with
15.5\% of permutation-null values below 0.05, which is why only exact
per-event permutation ranks are used for the primary result.

Two sensitivity analyses did not improve the event-level result. Testing
3,708 individual cell-type pairs gave 97 FDR-significant pair hypotheses but
only 32 distinct partitions after correction across all pairs. Restricting
each block to its two molecule-weighted dominant paths increased coverage;
with a 1\% path floor it gave 88 discoveries, and removing the floor gave 91,
under a cross-fitted empirical null. These analyses support the full-path
exact omnibus test as the primary cell-type analysis.

Allowing the alternative to re-estimate \(\kappa\) also left the result
unchanged: it found the same 101 partitions in the same 95 genes, with no
event entering or leaving the 5\% FDR set. Exact p-values from fixed- and
free-concentration fits had Spearman correlation 0.9995, and both had 148
nominal events. The null optimum was
multinomial for 499 of 646 tests, and the alternative optimum was
multinomial for 580. Among 66 tests with finite concentration in both models,
the median alternative/null concentration ratio was 1.54, consistent with
cell-type mean effects explaining some apparent overdispersion. Exact
permutation calibration absorbs this nuisance-parameter flexibility, so
freeing \(\kappa\) provides no net power gain here.

\subsection*{Calibration}

Across the 25-UMI analysis, 2.7\% of permuted-label Wald p-values were below
0.05 for both conditional and profile covariance, indicating conservative
null calibration. A parametric bootstrap of 3,000 reliable local fits gave
mean squared standardized error 1.10 and 94.5\% coverage for nominal 95\%
intervals. Independent Gaussian simulations of REML--GLS gave type-I error
between 2.7\% and 5.0\% across the tested biological-variance settings.

Numerical tests additionally verify skipped-exon block construction,
identifiable covariance after collapsing indistinguishable transcripts,
rejection of information-null path contrasts, the analytic binomial Fisher
variance, and recovery of imposed GLS effects.

\subsection*{Coverage-cutoff sensitivity}

\[
\begin{array}{rrrrrr}
\toprule
\text{UMIs} & \text{blocks} & \text{tests} &
\text{conditional null }p<.05 & \text{profile null }p<.05 &
\text{bootstrap coverage}\\
\midrule
25  & 10100 & 1335 & 0.027 & 0.027 & 0.945\\
50  &  5896 &  755 & 0.031 & 0.030 & 0.936\\
100 &  2820 &  283 & 0.029 & 0.025 & 0.946\\
200 &  1034 &   69 & 0.028 & 0.026 & 0.948\\
500 &   195 &   10 & 0.030 & 0.050 & 0.946\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
No cutoff produced an FDR-significant block. The 500-UMI calibration estimate
is based on only 100 permutation tests.

For reliable two-condition, two-path conditional tests, mean asymptotic power
for a 0.5 ILR effect was 0.49, 0.51, 0.44, 0.56, and 0.59 at increasing
cutoffs. The final two values are selected from only eight and two eligible
tests. Among 103 hypotheses shared by the 25- and 50-UMI analyses, power was
0.453 and 0.449. Lower coverage cutoffs therefore increase the number of
testable blocks without degrading calibrated precision among shared tests.
The 25-UMI cutoff is the default.

\subsection*{Does cell resolution increase condition-test power?}

The cell-resolved likelihood was run on the 527 blocks testable in the
25-UMI primary analysis. All 41,242 block-by-pseudobulk fits converged;
21,624 passed the reliability rules, compared with 21,010 pseudobulk
conditional estimates on the same blocks. This produced 836 condition tests,
compared with 829 for pseudobulk fitting. No cell-resolved test passed 5\%
FDR. Condition-label permutations were conservative, with 2.49\% of null
p-values below 0.05.

An exact matched comparison used the same reliable samples for both methods
in 807 block-by-cell-type tests. Cell resolution reduced the median local
covariance trace by 1.6\%, and the median path-response RMS difference was
0.033 ILR units. P-values were strongly concordant (Spearman rank
correlation 0.958); 24 pseudobulk and 25 cell-resolved tests were
nominally significant.

For the 259 matched two-condition, two-path tests, mean asymptotic power for
a 0.5 ILR effect was 0.504 for both methods. The median ratio of
cell-resolved to pseudobulk condition-coefficient standard error was 0.995.
Retaining cell likelihood terms therefore did not materially increase power
in this dataset. The aggregated EC counts and decoder baseline preserve
nearly all relevant local information within a cell type, while residual
between-mouse variation dominates the small reduction in inferential
variance. Pseudobulk fitting remains the default; the cell-resolved method is
available for datasets with stronger within-pseudobulk transcript
heterogeneity.

\subsection*{Compositional and joint-test assessment}

Collapsing equivalent transcript partitions reduced the 829 conditional
block-by-cell-type rows to 505 nonredundant tests over 317 partitions. The
median effective path count was 32 molecules, or 62\% of gene UMIs; only
0.6\% of estimates reached the gene-UMI cap.

On the same 505 tests, conditional GLS had 12 nominal results and the
Dirichlet--multinomial had 18. Neither had a 5\% FDR discovery. The
Dirichlet--multinomial was conservatively calibrated: 3.76\% of 10,100
permuted-label p-values were below 0.05. In contrast, pure multinomial
regression gave 197 nominal and 155 FDR results, but 39.1\% of its permutation
p-values were below 0.05. UMI sampling alone therefore cannot represent the
large between-mouse compositional variation. Output tables mark pure
multinomial and naive shared-effect rows as invalid for inference while
retaining them as calibration diagnostics.

ACAT across cell types produced 8 nominal GLS and 12 nominal
Dirichlet--multinomial partitions among 317 tests, with no FDR discoveries.
Combining distinct partitions within genes gave 7 and 11 nominal genes among
276 tests, again with no FDR discoveries.

Naive shared-effect pooling illustrates the pseudoreplication risk. It gave
43 nominal and one FDR Dirichlet--multinomial partition, but synchronized
mouse permutations rejected 13.2\% of nulls at 0.05. The apparent discovery
had asymptotic \(p=6.4\times10^{-5}\) but permutation \(p=0.143\). Naive
shared GLS was also inflated, with 8.4\% null rejection.

The mouse-clustered shared GLS restored conservative calibration: 2.19\% of
7,169 synchronized permutation p-values were below 0.05. It produced 11
nominal partitions among 359 tests and no FDR discoveries; its smallest
p-value was 0.0126 with FDR 0.86. Gene-level ACAT gave 9 nominal genes among
313 and no FDR discoveries. Thus the calibrated gain is limited to the
increase from 12 to 18 nominal within-cell-type tests under the
Dirichlet--multinomial. None of the evaluated strategies creates a
genome-wide condition discovery in this dataset.

\section*{Limitations}

The profile covariance is conditional on the fitted genome-wide decoder.
Uncertainty in shared decoder parameters is expected to be small for common
paths but may matter for rare genes or cell types. A mouse-level jackknife of
the hierarchical fit is the preferred extension.

The current biological variance is isotropic in log-ratio space. Estimating a
full covariance separately for every block would be unstable with few mice.
A later empirical-Bayes model could shrink path-specific biological
covariances across genes.
