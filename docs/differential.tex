\section*{Overview}

\subsection*{Questions and unit of analysis}

The analysis asks two distinct questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Does local splice-path usage differ by experimental condition within a cell type?
  \item Does local splice-path usage differ across cell types within the same subjects?
\end{enumerate}
The primary analysis aggregates cells into \emph{subject-by-condition-by-cell-type pseudobulks} and estimates local splice-path compositions from their observed paired-primer equivalence-class (EC) counts. Biological replication is represented by subject; cells are never treated as independent condition replicates.

A splice block is a local region between constitutive exonic anchors. Transcripts that take the same path through that region are collapsed to one subisoform. This targets local splicing choices, which can be identifiable even when the corresponding full-length isoforms are not.

For condition effects, each hypothesis is indexed by a splice block and a cell type. It is an omnibus test of \[
  H_0:\quad\text{mean path usage is equal across the included conditions}.
\]
Only conditions represented by at least three subject pseudobulks are included. With two conditions and two paths, this is a subject-level differential path-log-ratio test. With more conditions or paths, all condition coefficients and path log-ratios are tested jointly.

For cell-type effects, the primary hypothesis is indexed by a nonredundant splice-path partition and jointly tests whether path usage differs among the represented cell types after blocking by subject. Pairwise subject-matched tests are secondary analyses used to localize omnibus signals and to match the hypotheses available from external comparators. A comparator pair must be represented in at least four subjects. Condition tests require at least 10 modeled gene UMIs per retained pseudobulk; the comparator benchmark uses 25 UMIs for alignment with the external-method screens. The cell-type and condition hypothesis families are reported separately.

\subsection*{Workflow}

The primary analysis proceeds in five steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Construct local splice blocks from the isoform annotation and map every supported isoform to a local path.
  \item Aggregate paired oligo(dT) and random-hexamer EC counts within each subject, condition, and cell type while retaining separate primer maps.
  \item For each block, construct a label-independent pooled isoform baseline and estimate only its $d_b=S_b-1$ local path-log-ratio perturbations while preserving within-path isoform mixtures and nuisance isoforms.
  \item Stabilize local path estimates with two pseudo-observations per path, then fit a subject-blocked omnibus cell-type model or a condition model within one cell type.
  \item Calibrate the resulting statistics using \(R_{\rm null}\) design-matched Monte Carlo null replicates with a leave-one-event-out empirical null CDF, then apply BH separately to cell-type and condition hypotheses. The default is \(R_{\rm null}=32\).
\end{enumerate}

The same Tealeaf local-path estimator is used for the comparator benchmark and full-data analyses. It retains both primer-specific EC observations and estimates the identifiable local path coordinates directly. Supporting and deprecated analyses appear in the appendices.

\section*{Notation and Dimensions}

An \emph{equivalence class} (EC) is a set of isoforms compatible with one UMI. An EC count is therefore observed, whereas its allocation among compatible isoforms is latent. A \emph{splice path} is an ordered local exon chain between two constitutive anchors. A \emph{subisoform} is the set of full-length isoforms taking the same path through one block. PSI means percent spliced in; for two paths it is one component of the path-composition vector. ILR means isometric log-ratio, an orthonormal coordinate system for a composition.

Let \(g=g(b)\) denote the gene containing block \(b\), and let \(\mathcal I_g\) be the set of isoforms retained by the paired-primer model and assigned to gene \(g\) by the isoform-to-gene table. Define \(T_g=|\mathcal I_g|\in\mathbb N\). This is a gene-level set, not the set of isoforms whose exons overlap the block, and it is therefore shared by every block of the gene. Let \(\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\subseteq\mathcal I_g\) be the retained, annotated spliced isoforms represented in the block's GTF-derived path map. Isoforms in \(\mathcal I_g\setminus\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\), including modeled unspliced precursors, remain nuisance isoforms in the EC likelihood.

Let \(I_b,S_b\in\mathbb N\) be the numbers of qualifying pseudobulks and represented spliced paths for block \(b\), with \(S_b\geq2\). Then \(d_b=S_b-1\in\mathbb N\) is its number of independent path contrasts. There are \(P=2\) primer protocols, and primer \(p\) has \(K_{gp}\in\mathbb N\) ECs for gene \(g\). The principal symbols in the primary model are listed below. \[
\begin{array}{lll}
\toprule
\text{symbol} & \text{dimension or domain} & \text{meaning}\\
\midrule
b & \{1,\ldots,\mathcal B\} & \text{splice-block index}\\
i & \{1,\ldots,I_b\} & \text{pseudobulk index within block }b\\
u & \{1,\ldots,U_b\} & \text{subject index within block }b\\
c & \{1,\ldots,C_b^{\rm cell}\} & \text{cell-type index within block }b\\
p & \{1,\ldots,P\} & \text{primer-protocol index}\\
t & \{1,\ldots,T_g\} & \text{isoform index within gene }g(b)\\
s & \{1,\ldots,S_b\} & \text{splice-path index}\\
\bm c_{bucp} & \mathbb N_0^{K_{gp}} & \text{observed subject--cell-type EC counts}\\
A_{gp} & \mathbb R_+^{K_{gp}\times T_g} & \text{weighted EC-by-isoform map}\\
\overline{\bm\theta}_g & \Delta^{T_g-1} & \text{label-independent pooled isoform baseline}\\
\bm\delta_{bi} & \mathbb R^{d_b} & \text{local path-log-ratio perturbation}\\
\bm\psi_{bi} & \Delta^{S_b-1} & \text{represented local path composition}\\
\bm y_{bi} & \mathbb R^{d_b} & \text{ILR coordinates of }\bm\psi_{bi}\\
Q_b & \mathbb R^{S_b\times d_b} & \text{Helmert ILR basis}\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Here \(\mathcal B\in\mathbb N\) is the number of blocks, \(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,\ldots\}\), and \(\Delta^{S_b-1}=\{\bm v\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b}:\bm1^\prime\bm v=1\}\). Bold lower-case symbols are vectors, upper-case symbols are matrices, \(\bm1_k\in\mathbb R^k\) is a vector of ones, \(\bm0_k\in\mathbb R^k\) is a vector of zeros, and \(I_k\in\mathbb R^{k\times k}\) is the identity.

\subsection*{Helmert basis and ILR coordinates}

The simplex has only \(d_b=S_b-1\) free directions because path proportions sum to one. The Helmert matrix \(Q_b\) is a fixed orthonormal basis for the sum-zero subspace: \[
 Q_b^\prime Q_b=I_{d_b},\qquad Q_b^\prime\bm1_{S_b}=\bm0_{d_b}.
\]
Using one-based row \(r\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\) and column \(j\in\{1,\ldots,d_b\}\), its entries are \[
 (Q_b)_{rj}=
 \begin{cases}
  1/\sqrt{j(j+1)}, & r\leq j,\\
  -j/\sqrt{j(j+1)}, & r=j+1,\\
  0, & r>j+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
For any strictly positive \(\bm\psi_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\), the ILR coordinate is \(\bm y_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), where the logarithm is elementwise. For \(S_b=2\), \(Q_b=(1,-1)^\prime/\sqrt2\) and \(y_{bi}=\log(\psi_{bi1}/\psi_{bi2})/\sqrt2\). Thus a two-path ILR test is a scaled log-odds, or differential-PSI, test.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Tealeaf}

\subsubsection*{Local-path estimation}

Fix a gene \(g\) and one of its splice blocks \(b\). For subject \(u\), cell-type level \(c\), and primer protocol \(p\in\{1,2\}\), let \(\bm c_{bucp}\in\mathbb N_0^{K_{gp}}\) be the observed EC-count vector and let \(A_{gp}\in\mathbb R_+^{K_{gp}\times T_g}\) be the fixed EC-by-isoform compatibility or conditional-weight matrix. Oligo(dT) and random-hexamer observations have separate count vectors and matrices, so primer-specific positional coverage enters the likelihood without changing the underlying isoform composition. Counts from the same subject and cell type are summed within primer. Conditioning on each observed primer total removes library size from the composition likelihood. An EC may be compatible with multiple isoforms; its count is not first allocated to a transcript or path. Unsupported isoforms and ECs not assigned to \(g\) are excluded, but every supported isoform of \(g\), including an unspliced nuisance isoform, remains in every block likelihood for that gene.

After defining the observations eligible for a test, the baseline is estimated from their EC counts and compatibility matrices without using the labels within that set. Let \(\mathcal I_b\) index the subject--cell-type observations included for block \(b\), and define the primer-specific pooled count vector \(\overline{\bm c}_{bp}=\sum_{(u,c)\in\mathcal I_b}\bm c_{bucp}\in\mathbb N_0^{K_{gp}}\). The label-independent pooled isoform composition \(\overline{\bm\theta}_g\in\Delta^{T_g-1}\) is the joint conditional-multinomial maximum-likelihood estimate \[\overline{\bm\theta}_g=\arg\max_{\bm\theta\in\Delta^{T_g-1}}\sum_{p=1}^2\overline{\bm c}_{bp}^{\mathsf T}\log\left(\frac{A_{gp}\bm\theta}{\bm1^{\mathsf T}A_{gp}\bm\theta}\right).\] Dependence on \(\mathcal I_b\) is suppressed in the notation. The implementation uses reference-logit coordinates, starts from equal isoform weights, and optimizes this low-dimensional objective by L-BFGS. Primer counts are pooled separately, so their distinct compatibility and positional-coverage maps remain in the objective, while conditioning on each primer total removes library-size differences. The estimate is computed once for each gene and included observation set and reused across blocks sharing both; because neither observed nor permuted labels enter its estimation, it is unchanged in the matched null analyses.

Let \(Q_b\in\mathbb R^{S_b\times d_b}\) be the Helmert basis defined above. Let \(r_b(t)\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\cup\{\varnothing\}\) map isoform \(t\) to its represented path or mark it as a nuisance isoform, let \(\mathcal R_b=\{t:r_b(t)\ne\varnothing\}\), and let \(\overline\rho_b=\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_b}\overline\theta_{gt}\in(0,1]\) be the pooled mass of represented isoforms. Thus \(\overline{\bm\theta}_g\) supplies the fixed within-path isoform ratios, nuisance-isoform abundances, and represented mass around which each subject--cell-type local path fit is perturbed.

For any \(\bm\delta\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), define the perturbed isoform composition \(\bm\theta_g(\bm\delta)\in\Delta^{T_g-1}\) componentwise by \[\theta_{gt}(\bm\delta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle \overline\rho_b\frac{\overline\theta_{gt}\exp\{Q_{b,r_b(t)}^{\mathsf T}\bm\delta\}}{\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_b}\overline\theta_{gv}\exp\{Q_{b,r_b(v)}^{\mathsf T}\bm\delta\}},&t\in\mathcal R_b,\\ \overline\theta_{gt},&t\notin\mathcal R_b.\end{cases}\] Define its represented path composition \(\bm\psi_b(\bm\delta)\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\) by \[\psi_{bs}(\bm\delta)=\overline\rho_b^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_b:r_b(t)=s}\theta_{gt}(\bm\delta),\qquad s\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}.\] Thus \(\bm\delta\) changes only local path usage, while within-path isoform ratios, nuisance isoforms, and total represented mass remain fixed.

For subject--cell-type combination \((u,c)\), Tealeaf maximizes the two-primer EC log likelihood with \(\alpha_{\rm path}=2\) pseudo-observations on every path, \[\widehat{\bm\delta}_{buc}=\arg\max_{\bm\delta\in\mathbb R^{d_b}}\left\{\sum_{p=1}^2\bm c_{bucp}^{\mathsf T}\log\left(\frac{A_{gp}\bm\theta_g(\bm\delta)}{\bm1^{\mathsf T}A_{gp}\bm\theta_g(\bm\delta)}\right)+\alpha_{\rm path}\sum_{s=1}^{S_b}\log\psi_{bs}(\bm\delta)\right\},\qquad\alpha_{\rm path}=2.\] Thus the reported production fit has total prior concentration \(S_b\alpha_{\rm path}\), which increases with the number of represented paths. The alternative dimension-invariant parameterization assigns total concentration \(A\geq0\), or \(A/S_b\) pseudo-observations per path under uniform centering. The penalty stabilizes boundary estimates. Junction, exonic, and ambiguous reads contribute through their compatibility patterns without first assigning reads to global isoforms.

\subsubsection*{Statistical testing}

Let \(\widehat{\bm y}_{buc}=Q_b^{\mathsf T}\log\widehat{\bm\psi}_{buc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\). The primary cell-type analysis uses every fitted subject--cell-type vector for a block in one multivariate regression. Let \(N_b^{\rm obs}\in\mathbb N\), \(U_b\in\mathbb N\), and \(C_b^{\rm cell}\in\mathbb N\) be its numbers of subject--cell-type observations, subjects, and cell types, and stack the responses as \(Y_b\in\mathbb R^{N_b^{\rm obs}\times d_b}\). The design \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{N_b^{\rm obs}\times(U_b+C_b^{\rm cell}-1)}\) contains an intercept, \(U_b-1\) subject indicators, and \(C_b^{\rm cell}-1\) cell-type indicators. The equal-weight model is \[Y_b=X_b\Gamma_b+E_b,\qquad E_{bn}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}\sim N(\bm0_{d_b},\tau_b^2I_{d_b}),\] where \(\Gamma_b\in\mathbb R^{(U_b+C_b^{\rm cell}-1)\times d_b}\) and \(\tau_b^2\geq0\). The joint Wald test sets the \((C_b^{\rm cell}-1)\times d_b\) cell-type coefficient block to zero and therefore has at most \((C_b^{\rm cell}-1)d_b\) degrees of freedom. Subject indicators preserve the repeated-measures design while producing one cell-type hypothesis per block. With zero conditional measurement covariance, the REML scalar-variance generalized least-squares model reduces exactly to multivariate ordinary least squares, and the implementation uses this closed form.

For a condition test within one cell type, the subject-level vectors enter an equal-weight multivariate regression with an intercept and \(C_b^{\rm cond}-1\) condition columns, where \(C_b^{\rm cond}\in\mathbb N\) is the number of represented conditions. An isotropic between-subject variance is fitted by REML under the null, and all condition-by-path coefficients are tested jointly.

The secondary pairwise cell-type analysis localizes differences and supplies Tealeaf hypotheses matched to junction-based comparators. For cell types 1 and 2, define the paired subject contrast \(\widehat{\bm d}_{bu}=\widehat{\bm y}_{bu2}-\widehat{\bm y}_{bu1}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\). A paired \(t\) test is used when \(d_b=1\), and Hotelling's test is used when \(d_b>1\). Scalar sampling variances are moderated across blocks with a robust scaled-inverse-chi-squared prior, refitted inside every null family.

Let \(R_{\rm null}\in\mathbb N\) be the number of Monte Carlo null replicates, with default \(R_{\rm null}=32\). For every block and replicate, the null transformation is a within-subject cell-type-label permutation for the primary omnibus regression, a condition-label permutation for a condition contrast, or a paired subject sign flip for a pairwise cell-type contrast. Collecting replicate \(r\in\{1,\ldots,R_{\rm null}\}\) across all blocks gives one null p-value family on which the complete BH procedure can be audited. Calibration pools the null reference p-values across blocks only within matched tested-dimension and subject-count strata. If \(\widetilde p_b\in[0,1]\) is block \(b\)'s uncalibrated reference p-value, \(\widetilde p_{jr}^{(0)}\in[0,1]\) is block \(j\)'s null reference p-value in replicate \(r\), and \(\mathcal S_b\) is the set of blocks in its calibration stratum, the leave-one-block-out calibrated value is \[p_b=\frac{1+\sum_{j\in\mathcal S_b\setminus\{b\}}\sum_{r=1}^{R_{\rm null}}\mathbf1\{\widetilde p_{jr}^{(0)}\leq\widetilde p_b\}}{1+R_{\rm null}(|\mathcal S_b|-1)}.\] Thus the calibration CDF contains approximately \(R_{\rm null}|\mathcal S_b|\) values rather than only \(R_{\rm null}\), while the whole-family BH audit still contains only \(R_{\rm null}\) realizations. The default 32 is a computational choice rather than a theoretically distinguished value; sensitivity to 64 and 128 replicates is reported below. BH is applied separately to the cell-type and condition families. Supporting analyses of path-estimate uncertainty, alternative smoothing, and the deprecated global EC GLMM are reported in the appendix.

\subsection*{Comparators}

The same biological subjects are divided into two condition-balanced halves for Tealeaf and every comparator. The benchmark retains the 25-modeled-gene-UMI candidate screen and requires at least four subjects for each cell-type pair; Tealeaf tests 7,671 and 5,140 candidate block-by-cell-type-pair hypotheses in the two halves. Spliced alignments supplied to the external methods are restricted to unique genomic mappings and deduplicated by cell barcode, UMI, genomic start, and CIGAR. LeafCutter tests intron clusters, MAJIQ tests edges within local splicing variations, and scQuint tests intron groups. For each Tealeaf--comparator pair, scoring retains only genes and cell-type pairs eligible for both methods in both halves. Feature p-values are combined by Simes within each gene--cell-type pair and then across the shared pairs within a gene. For method \(m\), gene \(g\), and half \(h\in\{1,2\}\), let \(p_{mgh}\in[0,1]\) be the resulting gene p-value. The conjunction p-value \(p_{mg}^{\rm rep}=\max(p_{mg1},p_{mg2})\) tests whether the gene has signal in both halves, and BH at 0.05 is applied on the pairwise shared gene universe. Secondary endpoints are held-half nominal replication and Spearman correlation of gene--pair \(-\log_{10}p\) across halves.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Tealeaf reproducibility against comparator methods}

Table~\ref{tab:ec-glmm-reproducibility} gives the calibrated direct local-path comparison. Tealeaf has more independently replicated genes than every comparator, 50 versus 26 for LeafCutter, 103 versus 38 for relaxed-coverage MAJIQ Heterogen, and 101 versus 56 for scQuint. Its held-half nominal replication and fold-to-fold rank correlation are also higher in all three comparisons. The advantage is therefore not a consequence of admitting a larger unstable discovery set. It is consistent with the intended information gain from combining junction, exonic, and ambiguous EC reads while testing the \(S_b-1\) local path coordinates rather than a larger set of junction coefficients. Because \(\alpha_{\rm path}=2\) was selected after a smoothing sensitivity on these subject splits, the numerical advantage is internally validated rather than an untouched external benchmark and requires confirmation in an independent dataset.

The fold-specific Tealeaf analyses make 1,163 and 770 BH calls among 7,523 and 5,057 eligible block tests. Their calibrated sign-flip null rejection rates are respectively 0.05004 and 0.05001 at nominal 0.05, 0.01000 and 0.01003 at nominal 0.01, and 0.00100 in both folds at nominal 0.001. No fold-specific null family makes a BH call among the default 32 replicates. Propagating the null p-values through the same within-pair Simes aggregation, across-pair gene aggregation, two-fold conjunction, and BH procedure produces zero replicated genes in every one of these 32 null families on each comparator-matched universe. In the nested \(R_{\rm null}=64\) and 128 sensitivity, Tealeaf's replicated-gene counts are respectively 50 and 50 against LeafCutter, 104 and 103 against MAJIQ Heterogen, and 98 and 99 against scQuint, compared with 50, 103, and 101 at the default. The complete replication pipeline produces zero null replicated genes in all 128 families on every comparator-matched universe.

The 192 one-core fitting shards use 2.516 aggregate worker hours across both folds, with maximum shard elapsed time 78 seconds and peak worker resident memory 0.288 GiB. Merge-time moderation and empirical calibration add less than two minutes of serial work. This is substantially cheaper than repeatedly fitting the nested latent EC GLMM and shows that preserving primer-specific EC compatibility does not itself require the larger computational cost of the global random-effects model.

The smoothing sensitivity in Table~\ref{tab:path-alpha-sensitivity} holds variance moderation, empirical calibration, gene aggregation, and the two-fold replication endpoint fixed while varying only the uniform per-path pseudocount. \subsection*{Empirical-Bayes smoothing and path-estimate uncertainty}

Two extensions were evaluated before fixing the production second stage. First, smoothing strength was estimated with a testing-level empirical-Bayes model that uses the same uniform-centered fixed-total path estimator as the downstream test. Let \(p_j(A)\in[0,1]\) be the empirically calibrated p-value for test \(j\) at total concentration \(A\in\{2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}\). The two-groups marginal density is \(f_A(p)=\pi_{0,A}+(1-\pi_{0,A})a_Ap^{a_A-1}\), where \(\pi_{0,A}\in(0,1)\) is the null weight and \(a_A\in(0,1)\) is the shape of a decreasing beta alternative. For each candidate \(A\), \(\pi_{0,A}\) and \(a_A\) maximize the average log density within gene and then across genes, so genes with more blocks or cell-type pairs do not dominate. One global concentration is selected by \(\widehat A=\arg\max_A G^{-1}\sum_{g=1}^G n_g^{-1}\sum_{j:g(j)=g}\log f_A\{p_j(A)\}\), where \(G\in\mathbb N\) is the number of genes, \(n_g\in\mathbb N\) is the number of tests for gene \(g\), and \(g(j)\) maps test \(j\) to its gene. This is empirical Bayes for the tested null/non-null mixture rather than predictive evidence for individual count vectors, and it requires no fitting-data split.

Pooling the two assessment halves selects \(\widehat A=32\); fitting them separately gives 32 and 64. The evidence curve increases from 0.190 at \(A=2\) to 0.333 at \(A=32\), then decreases to 0.309 at \(A=256\). To include hyperparameter selection in the calibration audit, the full selector was rerun independently on each of the 32 matched null families. All selected \(A=2\), their mean rejection rates were 0.04995 in both halves, and none made a fold-level BH call. At \(A=32\), Tealeaf makes 1,471 and 1,047 fold BH calls and has 73/156/160 replicated genes on the LeafCutter, MAJIQ Heterogen, and scQuint matched universes. This exceeds the 68/139/145 replicated genes obtained by the earlier baseline-evidence transfer while removing its center mismatch.

The earlier count-predictive EB analysis is retained as a diagnostic. A baseline-centered spike-and-slab count model selected fixed total concentration 16, but applying only that scalar to uniform-centered testing was not a coherent transfer of one prior. Applying a common baseline-centered prior in both stages did not preserve power: an isotropic Gaussian prior on ILR perturbations selected precision 32 and produced only 23 and 11 fold BH calls, while a heavier-tailed symmetric Dirichlet prior on relative path fold changes selected concentration 64 and produced only 31 and 14. Both had pooled null rejection near 0.05 and no null-family BH calls. Shrinking every subject--cell-type composition toward one common biological center therefore attenuates the contrasts, regardless of local curvature or tail weight. Uniform-centered count-predictive evidence instead selected concentrations 2--4 and also lost replication. The testing-level two-groups model is the preferred EB sensitivity because selection and testing use the same uniform-centered estimator and null/non-null target.

Second, if \(I_{bmc}\in\mathbb S_+^{S_b-1}\) is the expected EC likelihood information and \(C(\bm\psi)=\operatorname{diag}(\bm\psi)-\bm\psi\bm\psi^{\mathsf T}\), the conditional Laplace covariance was estimated as \[V_{bmc}=\left\{I_{bmc}+\alpha_bS_bB_b^{\mathsf T}C(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bmc})B_b\right\}^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{S_b-1}.\] For a paired contrast, \(W_{bm}=V_{bm1}+V_{bm2}\in\mathbb S_+^{S_b-1}\) and the measurement-error model was \(\widehat{\bm d}_{bm}\mid\bm d_{bm}\sim N(\bm d_{bm},\lambda W_{bm})\), \(\bm d_{bm}\sim N(\bm\mu_b,\tau_b^2I_{S_b-1})\), where \(\lambda\geq0\) is shared within cross-fitting strata and \(\tau_b^2\geq0\) is fitted by REML. The analogous condition model scaled each mouse's covariance before multivariate GLS.

At \(\lambda=1\), the fold-specific cell-type analyses made 684 and 524 BH calls, but independently replicated genes fell to 28, 10, and 11 against the three comparators. Cross-fitted REML profiles over \(\lambda\in\{0,0.001,0.003,0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3\}\) selected zero in every split, full cell-type, and full condition stratum. The conditional EC covariance therefore did not explain held-out mouse-to-mouse error beyond the fitted biological variance and harmed the discovery tail when imposed at unit scale. \subsection*{Pairwise Tealeaf sensitivity analyses}

The corrected tangent-null Laplace EC GLMM was first rerun on the complete 25-UMI pairwise candidate manifests. Against LeafCutter, relaxed-coverage MAJIQ Heterogen, and scQuint it had 12, 19, and 18 replicated genes, compared with 25, 38, and 57, while its held-half nominal replication rates were 0.867, 0.828, and 0.828. The global latent model therefore retained stable signals but did not realize the expected local-path power advantage.

The direct local-path estimator without smoothing had unstable boundary estimates. Table~\ref{tab:path-alpha-sensitivity} reports the final common-pipeline comparison of no smoothing and 0.5--128 pseudo-observations per path. The earlier native-test screens used to develop variance moderation are superseded by this comparison. Dropping nuisance isoforms and ECs with only nuisance support, combining globally and locally conditioned p-values, restricting to two-path blocks, and replacing Simes with Cauchy gene aggregation did not improve calibrated replication and were rejected. These analyses show that boundary stabilization and cross-block variance moderation, rather than discarding ambiguous EC information, account for the final power gain.

\subsection*{Depth-filtered split sensitivity}

Filtering the pairwise split benchmark at a median gene depth of 100 modeled UMIs reduces the jointly converged Tealeaf fits to 1,101 block-pair tests from 202 genes in fold 0 and 564 tests from 94 genes in fold 1. The intersections with LeafCutter, relaxed-coverage MAJIQ, and scQuint then contain only 172, 364, and 337 gene-pair tests from 36, 63, and 61 genes. Tealeaf has 4, 11, and 11 replicated genes on these intersections, compared with 9, 17, and 11 for the respective comparators. Although the retained 77-test label-swap subset has a 0.0519 nominal rejection rate and no p-value below 0.01, its resolution is too low and its coverage loss is too severe for this filter to define the main genome-wide comparison. It is retained only as a depth sensitivity.

Lowering the inclusion threshold from 25 to 10 modeled gene UMIs and replacing the separate block tests with one unrestricted isoform-composition test per gene and cell-type pair expands the two split manifests to 22,453 tests from 2,503 genes and 16,753 tests from 2,238 genes. On the shared universes, this joint-gene sensitivity has 23 versus 29 replicated genes against LeafCutter, 66 versus 35 against relaxed-coverage MAJIQ, and 53 versus 104 against scQuint. Its held-half nominal replication is 0.780, 0.839, and 0.842, exceeding the corresponding comparator rates of 0.684, 0.659, and 0.776. The larger universe therefore recovers reproducible signal and exceeds MAJIQ's discovery count, but it does not dominate the other comparators. More importantly, only 946 of 1,000 paired-label-swap fits jointly converge, 86, 21, and 6 have p-values below 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, and three survive BH at 0.05. This low-depth joint test is too anti-conservative for production.

We also tested the requested gene model in which every annotated block contributes an independent fixed-effect subspace, with linear dependencies among overlapping block contrasts removed before fitting. At the 25-UMI screen, its shared-universe replicated-gene counts are 12, 19, and 18 against LeafCutter, relaxed MAJIQ, and scQuint, below both the respective comparator counts of 24, 38, and 57 and the unrestricted joint-gene sensitivity on the same screen. \subsection*{Computational benchmarks}

Table~\ref{tab:method-resources} compares aggregate worker time and peak resident memory for the broad cell-type and condition campaigns. Aggregate worker time is the sum of elapsed time over analysis tasks, so it is independent of queue delay and parallelism. Peak memory is the maximum for one worker. The accounting starts from each method's prepared analysis input and includes model fitting and method-specific result construction, but excludes shared read processing. Tealeaf uses the same ordinary multinomial EC GLMM, closed-form pseudobulk likelihood derivatives, and Laplace LRT in both datasets. Each first attempt uses 12 posterior-mode Newton updates and implicit differentiation, failed-convergence fits receive a 30-update continuation, and compiled objectives are reused within bounded gene-and-shape groups. On a complete 72-test human condition shard, implicit differentiation reduced elapsed time from 79.7 to 37.1 minutes and peak memory from 6.88 to 6.66 GiB relative to differentiating through the Newton updates, with the same 69 jointly converged tests and median absolute statistic difference below \(10^{-12}\). Gene-group compilation reuse reduced a second shard benchmark from 37.9 to 37.1 minutes. Complete analytic-derivative cell-type and condition reruns used 51.0 mouse worker hours and 4.81 GiB peak worker memory, and 313.3 human worker hours and 8.25 GiB; each broad campaign is 3.3-fold slower than scQuint, compared with 7.8-fold before the derivative change. Human condition fitting uses finer shards than cell-type fitting because retaining compiled shapes across hundreds of heterogeneous condition tests in one process exceeds the worker memory budget; the mathematical model, initialization, optimizer, tolerances, and continuation are unchanged. The human run has 4.3-fold more tests than mouse and its median test has five rather than three isoforms, 26 rather than nine ECs, and five rather than two block paths; these larger likelihoods account for most of the remaining dataset difference. Lower-precision and early-stopping experiments did not preserve accuracy or reduce real-data elapsed time, so neither is used for the reported fits.

A matched optimization study retained L-BFGS as the production outer optimizer. Pure projected Adam at two learning rates failed the joint outer-gradient and posterior-mode convergence criteria on nearly every tested fit and changed some LRT statistics by hundreds; an Adam warm-up followed by L-BFGS recovered convergence but reached different local optima and changed statistics by as much as 3.79. Muon is not used because the fixed-effect vector has no natural matrix axis, its apparent matrix shape would couple unrelated mice or depend on the arbitrary block-contrast basis. Momentum SGD, RMSProp, Lion, and related fixed-schedule first-order methods share Adam's batching advantage but not L-BFGS's line search or curvature history, so the failed Adam experiment provides no reason to replace the converged optimizer with them. Projecting a previously fitted alternative into later null models reduced iterations but also moved local optima. Running two independent L-BFGS workers reduced matched wall time by 38\% for mouse and 24\% for human, with greater peak memory and little reduction in aggregate CPU time.

Genome-wide shape census supports batched likelihood evaluation but not one globally padded tensor. Among 4,825 mouse and 11,597 human block tests, only 813 and 434 belong to an exact tensor-shape group of size at least two. Allowing pseudobulk and mouse counts to vary while holding the isoform, EC, and fixed-effect dimensions constant raises these counts to 3,726 and 7,971. Cost-sorted padded batches incur estimated likelihood-work inflation of 1.97-fold for mouse and 2.11-fold for human at batch size eight, increasing with batch size. On representative low-padding batches, a padded likelihood-gradient kernel was 2.5-fold faster than a \texttt{ragged\_dot} kernel for mouse and 2.2-fold faster for human. On deliberately high-padding tails, the ragged kernel was 1.09-fold and 1.86-fold faster and compiled faster.

A full padded Laplace prototype gives every fit an independent L-BFGS history, projected gradient, active state, and Armijo line search while evaluating the separable objectives together. It preserves implicit differentiation of each posterior mode and reuse of a shared unrestricted alternative. In ordinary low-dimensional buckets, batches of eight were 5.48-fold faster for mouse and 6.43-fold faster for human than sequential scalar L-BFGS-B. All eight batched nested fits converged in each dataset, compared with seven mouse and eight human scalar fits. Among fits converged by both implementations, the maximum absolute LRT-statistic differences were 0.0090 and 0.00058. A four-fit high-dimensional mouse bucket was 1.53-fold faster after alternative reuse and gave identical statistics. A high-dimensional human bucket with ten isoforms, 18 null coefficients, 252 alternative coefficients, and roughly 900 pseudobulks was 0.62-fold as fast, meaning batching was 1.62-fold slower; the two commonly converged tests differed by 6.88 and 14.80 statistic units because the optimizers reached different local optima. Active masks preserve accepted fits but do not remove the arithmetic of inactive padded lanes from a compiled vectorized evaluation.

The initial selective policy was tested on every cell-type block, using cost-sorted batches of eight only for padding ratios at most two and nested models with at most 128 outer coefficients. The mouse hybrid reduced elapsed worker time only from 31.18 to 30.48 hours, while the human hybrid increased it from 98.39 to 125.21 hours. Peak worker memory fell from 3.97 to 1.40 GiB in mouse and from 6.55 to 4.19 GiB in human. The memory reduction did not compensate for the human slowdown or the movement between local optima. Production scalar fitting preserves bounded compilation reuse within a gene and covered pseudobulk set and initializes the first unrestricted alternative from its fitted null. The initial global shape-and-cost scheduler instead broke gene-local compilation reuse and fit deduplicated alternatives from their default initialization. The latter difference moved optima even on routes where both fits were scalar, while batching introduced additional trajectories. That initial hybrid was rejected without structured-null calibration because it failed the prior runtime and numerical-agreement gates.

A follow-up restored the production null-to-alternative initialization and made counts, EC maps, fixed-effect tensors, and cluster layouts dynamic arguments to a bounded global shape cache. On 20 real mouse tests, global caching reduced elapsed time by 10.3\% and objective-evaluation time by 9.7\% with identical statistics, at the cost of 11.0\% greater peak memory, so this bounded cache remains available. Reusing each accepted L-BFGS iterate's posterior mode with adaptive Newton termination halved optimizer time, but maximum statistic drift remained 0.319 at mode tolerance $10^{-5}$ and 0.187 at $10^{-8}$. A plug-in raw-EC efficient-score screen was slower than the full nested LRT because forming the observed outer Hessian dominated its cost; seven of 20 fixed-effect Hessians were indefinite, and one extreme score disagreed with the LRT tail. The production-initialized GPU scheduler later reduced mouse worker time by 21.3\% but increased human worker time by 36.9\%, increased peak memory in both datasets, and retained local-optimum drift. Adaptive mode reuse, the score screen, and batched schedulers were therefore removed rather than exposed as analysis options.

The multinomial EC likelihood permits a cheaper exact derivative kernel than nested automatic differentiation. For one pseudobulk and primer, let $\bm y\in\mathbb{N}^{E}$ contain counts for $E$ equivalence classes, let $M\in\{0,1\}^{E\times K}$ map those classes to $K$ isoforms, let $\bm a=\operatorname{softmax}([\bm\eta^T,0]^T)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ be isoform abundance for free logits $\bm\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{K-1}$, let $B=M\operatorname{diag}(\bm a)\in\mathbb{R}^{E\times K}$, let $\bm m=B\bm 1_K\in\mathbb{R}^{E}$, let $D=\bm 1_E^T\bm m$, let $N=\bm 1_E^T\bm y$, and let $\bm c=B^T\bm 1_E\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$. Ignoring the multinomial coefficient, the log likelihood is \[\ell=\bm y^T\log\bm m-N\log D.\] Its full-logit score $\bm g\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ and Hessian $H\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}$ are \[\bm g=B^T(\bm y\oslash\bm m)-N\bm c/D,\qquad H=\operatorname{diag}(\bm g)-B^T\operatorname{diag}(\bm y\oslash\bm m^2)B+N\bm c\bm c^T/D^2.\] Deleting the reference isoform's last matrix row and column gives derivatives with respect to $\bm\eta$, and derivatives sum over primers. Substituting these expressions for the pseudobulk-level autodiff score and Hessian leaves the Laplace objective, posterior-mode solve, implicit mode gradient, bounds, and L-BFGS convergence criteria unchanged while avoiding differentiation through an autodiff-generated Hessian.

On matched subsets of 12 mouse and 10 human tests, the analytic kernel reduced end-to-end time by factors of 1.49 and 1.48 and objective-evaluation time by factors of 1.24 and 1.48. Joint convergence was unchanged at 11 and 9 tests. Median absolute LRT differences were $2.4\times10^{-11}$ and $2.3\times10^{-13}$, and maxima were 0.040 and 0.124. Peak memory fell from 2.35 to 1.33 GiB in mouse and from 4.81 to 4.32 GiB in human.

\subsection*{Paired junction log-ratio baseline}

This appendix baseline was introduced to match the pairwise hypotheses and junction-feature universe used by the external methods. It does not use EC counts, primer-specific compatibility maps, isoform ambiguity, splice blocks, or latent Gaussian EC fitting and is therefore not labeled as Tealeaf.

For one junction group, let \(K\geq2\) be its number of retained introns and let \(n\) be the number of subjects represented exactly once in each of two cell types. For subject \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), cell type \(c\in\{0,1\}\), and intron \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), let \(y_{ick}\in\mathbb N_0\) be the pseudobulk junction count. Define \(\bm y_{ic}=(y_{ic1},\ldots,y_{icK})^\prime\in\mathbb N_0^K\), the centered log-ratio \[
 \operatorname{clr}(\bm y_{ic})
 =\log(\bm y_{ic}+1/2)
  -\frac{\bm1_K}{K}\bm1_K^\prime\log(\bm y_{ic}+1/2)
 \in\mathbb R^K,
\]
and the within-subject difference \(\bm d_i=H_K^\prime\{\operatorname{clr}(\bm y_{i1})- \operatorname{clr}(\bm y_{i0})\}\in\mathbb R^{K-1}\). Here \(H_K\in\mathbb R^{K\times(K-1)}\) is an orthonormal Helmert basis for the subspace orthogonal to \(\bm1_K\); the result is invariant to the particular orthonormal basis. Pairing removes every additive subject effect shared by the two cell types, while the log-ratio removes junction-group depth.

The null is \(H_0:\mathbb E(\bm d_i)=\bm0_{K-1}\). For \(K=2\), the baseline uses the exact two-sided one-sample Student \(t\) test. For \(K>2\), with \(q=K-1\), sample mean \(\bar{\bm d}\in\mathbb R^q\), and sample covariance \(S_d\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}\), it computes \[
 T^2=n\bar{\bm d}^{\prime}S_d^{-1}\bar{\bm d},\qquad
 \frac{n-q}{q(n-1)}T^2\sim F_{q,n-q}\quad\text{under }H_0.
\]
The test requires \(n\geq8\), \(n>q\), and full-rank \(S_d\). The eight-subject threshold was fixed from permutation calibration before the observed split comparison. Junction groups use the scQuint filters: each intron must occur in at least three samples per arm, have global proportion at least \(10^{-3}\), and belong to a nonsingleton group.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Paired junction log-ratio test for one cell-type pair}
\label{alg:paired-junction-logratio}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Count vectors \(\bm y_{ic}\in\mathbb N_0^K\) and subject labels.
\State Keep subjects with exactly one observation from each cell type and nonzero junction-group depth in both observations.
\State Apply the junction filters and require \(n\geq8\), \(n>K-1\).
\For{each retained subject \(i\)}
  \State Compute \(\bm d_i=H_K^\prime\{\operatorname{clr}(\bm y_{i1})- \operatorname{clr}(\bm y_{i0})\}\).
\EndFor
\If{\(K=2\)}
  \State Return the two-sided one-sample \(t\)-test p-value.
\Else
  \State Estimate \(S_d\); mark the feature ineligible if \(S_d\) is rank deficient.
  \State Return the exact Hotelling \(T^2\)-to-\(F\) p-value.
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Alternative Models and Supporting Derivations}

The main text defines the two EC-count GLMMs, the direct paired local-path likelihood, and their implemented tests. This appendix records additional path-estimate models, covariance approximations, alternative EC likelihoods, and inference benchmarks used to understand or validate those analyses.

\subsection*{Paired-primer EC likelihood}

For block \(b\), pseudobulk \(i\), and primer \(p\), define \(N_{bip}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm c_{bip}\in\mathbb N_0\), \(\bm q_{bip}=A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}}\), and \(Z_{bip}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm q_{bip}\in\mathbb R_+\). Conditional on the primer-specific UMI total, \[
 \bm c_{bip}\mid N_{bip},\bm\theta_{bi}
 \sim\operatorname{Multinomial}
 \left(N_{bip},\bm q_{bip}/Z_{bip}\right).
\]
The oligo(dT) and random-hexamer protocols use separate \(A_{bp}\) matrices and likelihood terms. Their different isoform exposures therefore model primer-specific positional coverage without forcing a common bias profile.

\subsection*{Why the former global basis lost local path power}

The former block test lifted an unweighted Helmert path contrast to global isoform logits and defined its nuisance space by Euclidean orthogonality. Let \(D_b\in\mathbb R^{T_g\times d_b}\) denote that centered lift and let \(N_b^{\rm old}\in\mathbb R^{r_g\times(r_g-d_b)}\) be the reference-logit image of its orthogonal complement. This construction ensures an unweighted inner product of zero, but the aggregate path ILR has the pooled-baseline derivative \(J_b\), whose entries depend on conditional isoform abundance within a path. In general, \[
 J_bN_b^{\rm old}\neq0.
\]
The old null could therefore change the aggregate local path balance that it was intended to hold fixed. This is a hypothesis-definition error rather than a missing-read problem. In the two subject halves, 1,081 of 1,353 and 653 of 859 eligible cassette tests have nonzero leakage. The median norms \(\|J_bN_b^{\rm old}\|_F/\|J_bH_b^{\rm old}\|_F\) are 0.341 and 0.330, the 90th percentiles are both 0.523, and the maxima are 0.817 and 0.816. In fold 0, leakage has Spearman correlation 0.236 with the corrected test's gain in \(-\log_{10}p\).

A second numerical issue arose because the unrestricted global alternative was cached for tests sharing a gene and covered pseudobulks. In the original cassette fits, a cached alternative had a worse optimized Laplace objective than a later block null for 232 of 1,302 and 137 of 820 jointly converged fits. The runner now detects a nesting violation, refines the shared alternative from the fitted block null, and retains the better converged optimum. This repair restores a valid nonnegative likelihood-ratio comparison but does not by itself produce the observed replication gain; changing the null geometry is necessary.

\subsection*{Pre-collapsed local-path comparator}

The path-space comparator reduces the latent dimension before fitting but retains the raw EC count vectors. Let \(T_g\in\mathbb N\) be the supported isoform count, \(S_b\in\mathbb N\) the represented local-path count, and \(U_b\in\mathbb N_0\) the number of isoforms retained only as normalization nuisances. It first estimates one label-independent pooled isoform mixture \(\widehat{\bm w}_g\in\Delta^{T_g-1}\) by maximizing the conditional EC log likelihood, \[\widehat{\bm w}_g=\arg\max_{\bm w\in\Delta^{T_g-1}}\sum_p\left\{\sum_{e=1}^{E_{gp}}\bar c_{gpe}\log(A_{gp}\bm w)_e-\bar N_{gp}\log(\bm1_{E_{gp}}^\prime A_{gp}\bm w)\right\},\] where \(\bar{\bm c}_{gp}=\sum_i\bm c_{gip}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{gp}}\) and \(\bar N_{gp}=\bm1_{E_{gp}}^\prime\bar{\bm c}_{gp}\in\mathbb N_0\). This pooling uses no cell-type or condition labels.

For represented path \(s\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\), let \(\mathcal I_{bs}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,T_g\}\) be its isoforms and define the fixed conditional mixture \(\widehat\rho_{bst}=\widehat w_{gt}/\sum_{u\in\mathcal I_{bs}}\widehat w_{gu}\) for \(t\in\mathcal I_{bs}\). The collapsed primer map \(B_{bp}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{gp}\times(S_b+U_b)}\) has represented-path columns \[(B_{bp})_{\mathbin\cdot s}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_{bs}}\widehat\rho_{bst}(A_{gp})_{\mathbin\cdot t}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{gp}},\] while every nuisance isoform retains its original column separately. If \(\bm\phi_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b+U_b}\) is the reduced latent abundance, the comparator replaces \(A_{gp}\bm\theta_{gi}\) by \(B_{bp}\bm\phi_{bi}\) and otherwise uses the same multinomial EC likelihood, subject random intercept, fixed-effect construction, Laplace fitting procedure, likelihood-ratio reference distribution, and BH correction as the isoform-space model.

Because counts are not assigned to paths before fitting, this construction retains the multinomial EC covariance \(N_{bip}\{\operatorname{diag}(\bm\pi_{bip})-\bm\pi_{bip}\bm\pi_{bip}^\prime\}\in\mathbb S_+^{E_{gp}}\) and propagates EC ambiguity through \(B_{bp}\). It does not retain isoform-specific information within a path. The reduction is exact only when the conditional isoform mixture is constant across pseudobulks and the fixed mixture is correct; otherwise it is a lower-dimensional working model. Nuisance isoforms remain separate specifically to avoid imposing one mixture on heterogeneous gene-level isoforms outside the tested block.

This reduction is small in the fitted universes. Only 29.7\% of mouse block tests and 12.8\% of human block tests contain two represented isoforms that can be collapsed to the same path. The mean fractional latent-dimension reductions are 10.6\% and 4.4\%, respectively. Among multi-isoform mouse path-by-primer groups in the two subject folds, only 0.22\% and 0.24\% have identical compatibility columns, confirming that the reduction is generally a working-model approximation rather than an exact reparameterization.

Table~\ref{tab:path-space-comparator} records the earlier multilevel and runtime ablation using the nested-autodiff Laplace implementation available at that time. Discovery is reported both on each method's converged tests and on the common-converged universe. Independent-subject replication requires a block test to converge for both methods in both folds, combines block p-values to genes by Simes, and applies BH to the two-fold conjunction p-value. The human sensitivity analysis uses 359 matched completed shards containing 4,282 tests and is not a genome-wide human analysis. The small dimension reduction did not improve runtime because block-specific maps prevented reuse of the unrestricted gene alternative.

The cassette-specific audit isolates the hypothesis geometry that the earlier gene aggregation obscured. The exact local-path model has 732 event-by-cell-type-pair tests converged in both subject halves, 27 are nominal in both halves, and six pass BH on the conjunction p-value. All six BH events and 25 of the 27 both-nominal events agree in effect direction. On 711 tests also converged under the former global basis in both halves, the exact local model has 26 both-nominal and four conjunction-BH events, compared with 11 and two. Among 971 cassette paired-label swaps, its rejection rates are 0.0587, 0.0113, and 0.00206 at nominal levels 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, with no BH calls. The former global model's completed native-pairwise null audit, which was not cassette-restricted, gave 0.0745, 0.0186, and 0.00414. The local collapse therefore recovers reproducible cassette signal without evidence that the gain comes from a looser reference distribution.

Discarding ambiguous observations is not responsible for this gain. In a 100-cassette strict audit, 24 tests lack a pure EC for at least one path. Among the 76 feasible tests, pure path-identifying ECs contain a median 96.5 UMIs, 16 ECs, and 10.7\% of crude gene UMI exposure. The strict likelihood gives 10 nominal tests, compared with 15 for the local-path likelihood on the same 76. Shared-exon and precursor-compatible reads are not automatically informative about cassette inclusion, but retaining the full EC vector lets every read contribute according to its primer-specific compatibility.

The tangent global-isoform model avoids fixing conditional isoform mixtures within paths and gives essentially the same cassette result, 28 both-nominal events, six conjunction-BH events, 26 of 28 signs agreeing, and effect Spearman correlation 0.722. Among 975 converged cassette paired-label swaps, its rejection rates are 0.0615, 0.0133, and 0.00205 at nominal levels 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, with no BH calls. It remains the corrected hypothesis geometry for the full-data EC GLMM. The direct local-path estimator is used for the primary pairwise comparator benchmark because it is faster and its calibrated subject-paired test has higher replication.

\subsection*{Estimate-once path Wald ablation}

This subsection records a rejected computational experiment. Its analytic reference failed the structured-null audit, so the estimate-once Wald implementation and its analysis launchers were removed; the result tables and findings remain for provenance.

The estimate-once ablation removes repeated raw-EC null and alternative GLMM fits. For each block \(b\), it uses the same pooled, label-independent isoform baseline \(\widehat{\bm w}_g\in\Delta^{T_g-1}\) defined above. For pseudobulk \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n_b\}\), it estimates one path perturbation \(\widehat{\bm\delta}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), where \(d_b=S_b-1\in\mathbb N\), by maximizing the two-primer conditional EC likelihood while holding the within-path isoform mixture fixed. The resulting path composition is \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\), its ILR response is \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), and the inverse conditional Fisher information gives \(V_{bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\). Thus the full off-diagonal covariance among path log ratios induced by the multinomial EC likelihood is retained; ECs are not assigned independently to paths.

The second stage uses the same nuisance and tested fixed-effect columns as the EC GLMM. Let \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times k_b}\) contain both sets of columns and \(m(i)\in\{1,\ldots,M_b\}\) map pseudobulk \(i\) to one of \(M_b\in\mathbb N\) mice. Define \(D_{bi}=X_{b,i\mathbin\cdot}\otimes I_{d_b}\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times k_bd_b}\), \(W_{bi}=V_{bi}^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\), and \(\bm\beta_b=\operatorname{vec}(\Beta_b)\in\mathbb R^{k_bd_b}\), where \(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k_b\times d_b}\). The precision-weighted estimate is \(\widehat{\bm\beta}_b=A_b^{-1}\sum_iD_{bi}^\prime W_{bi}\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\), where \(A_b=\sum_iD_{bi}^\prime W_{bi}D_{bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{k_bd_b}\). A pseudobulk whose EC information does not identify every tested path coordinate receives zero participation; a block is ineligible when the remaining pseudobulks do not span \(X_b\) or \(M_b\leq k_b\).

Mouse dependence and biological variation enter through a clustered sandwich covariance rather than a fitted latent Gaussian. For mouse \(m\), define its score \(\bm s_{bm}=\sum_{i:m(i)=m}D_{bi}^\prime W_{bi}(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}-D_{bi}\widehat{\bm\beta}_b)\in\mathbb R^{k_bd_b}\). The coefficient covariance is \(\widehat\Sigma_b=c_bA_b^{-1}(\sum_m\bm s_{bm}\bm s_{bm}^\prime)A_b^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{k_bd_b}\), where \(c_b\in\mathbb R_+\) is the standard cluster and residual degrees-of-freedom correction. If the tested covariance has rank \(q_b\in\mathbb N\), the reference distribution is \(W_b/q_b\sim F_{q_b,M_b-k_b}\), which reduces the small-cluster tail inflation of the chi-square reference.

This is a two-stage working model, not an equivalent reparameterization of the EC GLMM. It does not propagate uncertainty in \(\widehat{\bm w}_g\), assumes the local likelihood is adequately Gaussian on the ILR scale, freezes the observation covariance before estimating biological effects, and represents mouse dependence marginally rather than with a parametric random intercept. Its purpose is to measure the speed and empirical power available when raw EC ambiguity is resolved once rather than revisited inside every random-effect mode and optimizer iteration.

In the original nested-autodiff mouse cell-type ablation, the estimate-once test was eligible for 4,499 of 4,825 blocks and made 3,069 uncalibrated BH discoveries, a 68.2\% discovery rate, compared with 323 of 4,546 for the isoform-space EC GLMM and 354 of 4,579 for the collapsed-path EC GLMM. On the 4,211 blocks eligible for all three methods, their discovery counts were 303, 335, and 2,876. Of the 303 isoform-space discoveries, 276 were also selected by estimate-once Wald, but its much larger discovery set gave a Jaccard index of 0.095. In the subject split, 948 blocks and 724 genes were testable by all three methods. Isoform-space EC GLMM, collapsed-path EC GLMM, and estimate-once Wald produced 35, 40, and 276 genes significant in both halves; their held-half nominal replication rates were 0.669, 0.685, and 0.751, and their fold rank correlations were 0.429, 0.393, and 0.426. The structured-null audit below shows that these Wald discovery and block-replication counts are inflated and must not be interpreted as evidence of additional power.

On the human cell-type analysis, 8,062 of 11,597 blocks were eligible and 5,906 passed uncalibrated BH, a 73.3\% discovery rate, whereas the isoform-space EC GLMM was eligible for 11,003 blocks and selected 1,487, a 13.5\% rate. On 7,685 common-eligible blocks, the methods selected 1,170 and 5,633; 873 calls overlapped and the discovery-set Jaccard index was 0.147. The human eligibility loss comes mainly from requiring the complete local-path ILR vector and covariance to be identifiable in each retained pseudobulk, while the high discovery rate reflects the same null failure seen in mouse.

Including the small correction reruns used to enforce positive denominator degrees of freedom, estimate-once Wald used 2.78 aggregate worker hours and 0.54 GiB peak memory for mouse cell type, versus 12.57 hours and 4.00 GiB for the analytic-derivative isoform-space EC GLMM. Human values were 14.18 hours and 3.58 GiB, versus 55.17 hours and 8.25 GiB. The full external-method campaigns in Table~\ref{tab:method-resources} used 6.61--15.28 mouse worker hours and 9.55--94.09 human worker hours. Those campaigns cover different feature units and broader reported effect families, so they are contextual rather than matched denominators; nevertheless, resolving each EC likelihood once moves runtime into the comparator range. Retaining a dense \(d_b\times d_b\) covariance is therefore not the main cost. The primary EC GLMM remains slower because it repeatedly evaluates the raw EC likelihood and solves the latent Gaussian problem under both nested models.

The initial balanced two-level Gaussian null simulation did not reproduce the leverage and tested dimensions of the real design. The structured audit therefore holds every estimated \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\), \(V_{bi}\), nuisance column, and mouse membership fixed and independently shuffles the observed cell-type labels within each mouse. It also uses a restricted wild-cluster null, fitting the nuisance-only precision-weighted model and multiplying its complete multivariate residual vector by one Rademacher sign per mouse. Across 32 mouse null families, the CR1 analytic p-values rejected 65.8\%, 58.3\%, and 51.7\% of label permutations at nominal 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001. Wild-cluster rates were 60.3\%, 53.2\%, and 46.8\%. Each permuted family produced a mean 2,844 BH calls and each wild family a mean 2,581, compared with 3,069 in the observed data. Mapping observed analytic p-values through the matching null CDF within numerator-degree and cluster-degree strata left zero BH calls for either null construction.

A CR3 sensitivity premultiplies each whitened mouse score by the inverse cluster hat-matrix complement, rather than applying only the CR1 scalar correction. It reduced the observed mouse BH count from 3,069 to 1,906 but remained anti-conservative. Across 16 null families its nominal 0.05 rejection rates were 36.7\% for label permutations and 33.8\% for wild nulls, with mean BH counts 1,430 and 1,277. Stratified null calibration again left zero BH calls. The failure increases with the joint tested dimension, but CR3 one-degree-of-freedom tests still reject roughly 12--24\% under the structured null, so restricting to smaller blocks does not solve the problem.

An evenly spaced human sensitivity used 11 of 96 shards, 912 eligible observed blocks, and 16 null families. Label-permutation rejection was 75.3\%, 72.3\%, and 68.7\% at nominal 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001; wild-null rejection was 72.3\%, 68.8\%, and 65.2\%. The null families averaged 666 and 652 BH calls, and matched stratified calibration left zero. Thus the failure generalizes to human data and is stronger in its higher-dimensional tests.

Effect direction provides a separate check that does not depend on the analytic reference distribution. For each shared cell-type pair and local path, the two fold-specific ILR coefficient matrices were transformed to centered-log path effects. Across 985 blocks and 14,059 matched pair-by-path coordinates, signs agreed in 56.8\% and the fold effect Spearman correlation was 0.151. Coordinates in blocks passing uncalibrated BH in both folds had 57.7\% agreement and correlation 0.138, versus null sign agreement 51.0\% for label permutations and 50.2\% for wild draws. Requiring \(|z|\geq1.96\) in both folds raised agreement to 61.6\%, but only 1,035 coordinates from 279 blocks remained and their correlation was 0.173. Under CR3, the corresponding strong-coordinate agreement was 80.6\% among 217 coordinates from 69 blocks, but CR3 p-values themselves remain invalid. These results retain the runtime conclusion but reject estimate-once sandwich Wald as an inferential replacement for the EC GLMM.

\subsection*{Gaussian mixed-model LRT and score Bayes factors}

This subsection likewise records a rejected inferential experiment. Neither the LRT tail nor fixed Bayes-factor thresholds were calibrated well enough to replace the EC GLMM, so these implementations were removed from the package and analysis runners.

Two alternatives retain the estimate-once path responses but replace the clustered sandwich calculation. Stack the usable responses as \(\widehat{\bm y}_b\in\mathbb R^{n_bd_b}\), let \(Z_b\in\mathbb R^{n_bd_b\times a_b}\) be the nuisance design after taking its Kronecker product with \(I_{d_b}\), and let \(T_b\in\mathbb R^{n_bd_b\times q_b}\) be the corresponding tested design, where \(a_b\in\mathbb N\) and \(q_b\in\mathbb N\) are the nuisance and tested coefficient dimensions. The Gaussian random-intercept model is \[\widehat{\bm y}_b=Z_b\bm\alpha_b+T_b\bm\gamma_b+U_b\bm u_b+\bm\epsilon_b,\qquad \bm u_b\sim N(\bm0,\sigma_{m,b}^2I_{M_bd_b}),\qquad \bm\epsilon_b\sim N\{\bm0,\operatorname{blockdiag}_i(V_{bi}+\sigma_{e,b}^2I_{d_b})\},\] where \(U_b\in\{0,1\}^{n_bd_b\times M_bd_b}\) maps responses to mouse-specific \(d_b\)-vectors, \(\bm\alpha_b\in\mathbb R^{a_b}\), \(\bm\gamma_b\in\mathbb R^{q_b}\), \(\bm u_b\in\mathbb R^{M_bd_b}\), and \(\bm\epsilon_b\in\mathbb R^{n_bd_b}\). Consequently, responses from the same mouse have cross-pseudobulk covariance \(\sigma_{m,b}^2I_{d_b}\), while \(\sigma_{e,b}^2\in\mathbb R_+\) absorbs biological variation not represented by the conditional EC covariance \(V_{bi}\).

Let \(X_{0b}=Z_b\), \(X_{1b}=[Z_b\ T_b]\), and let \(\Omega_b(\sigma_m^2,\sigma_e^2)\in\mathbb S_{++}^{n_bd_b}\) be the marginal covariance implied by the model. For model \(h\in\{0,1\}\), maximum likelihood profiles its fixed effects as \(\widehat{\bm\xi}_{hb}=(X_{hb}^\prime\Omega_b^{-1}X_{hb})^{-1}X_{hb}^\prime\Omega_b^{-1}\widehat{\bm y}_b\) and separately optimizes both nonnegative variance components using the profile objective \[-2\ell_{hb}=\log|\Omega_b|+(\widehat{\bm y}_b-X_{hb}\widehat{\bm\xi}_{hb})^\prime\Omega_b^{-1}(\widehat{\bm y}_b-X_{hb}\widehat{\bm\xi}_{hb})+n_bd_b\log(2\pi).\] This is ML rather than REML because the nested models have different fixed-effect dimensions. The likelihood-ratio statistic is \(\Lambda_b=2(\ell_{1b}-\ell_{0b})\in\mathbb R_+\), with candidate reference \(\chi^2_{q_b}\), and the cluster-count BIC approximation is \(\log\mathrm{BF}^{\rm BIC}_{10,b}=\{\Lambda_b-q_b\log M_b\}/2\). Both variance components are refitted under the alternative, including boundary candidates with either or both components equal to zero.

The implementation does not form or factor a dense covariance for all pseudobulks from one mouse. Within mouse \(m\), let \(R_{bm}=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{i:m(i)=m}(V_{bi}+\sigma_{e,b}^2I_{d_b})\) and \(K_{bm}=\bm1\otimes I_{d_b}\). It applies \((R_{bm}+\sigma_{m,b}^2K_{bm}K_{bm}^\prime)^{-1}\) with the Woodbury identity and computes its determinant through the \(d_b\times d_b\) matrix \(I_{d_b}+\sigma_{m,b}^2K_{bm}^\prime R_{bm}^{-1}K_{bm}\). Runtime therefore scales with individual path-covariance factors and a path-dimensional correction rather than a cubic factorization in the number of pseudobulks per mouse.

The score Bayes factor requires only the fitted null covariance \(\widehat\Omega_{0b}\). Define the nuisance-residual precision \(P_{0b}=\widehat\Omega_{0b}^{-1}-\widehat\Omega_{0b}^{-1}Z_b(Z_b^\prime\widehat\Omega_{0b}^{-1}Z_b)^{-1}Z_b^\prime\widehat\Omega_{0b}^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{n_bd_b}\), the efficient score \(\bm s_b=T_b^\prime P_{0b}\widehat{\bm y}_b\in\mathbb R^{q_b}\), and its information \(\mathcal J_b=T_b^\prime P_{0b}T_b\in\mathbb S_{++}^{q_b}\). For a proper alternative prior \(\bm\gamma_b\sim N(\bm0,W_{bs})\), the local Gaussian integral is \[\log\mathrm{BF}_{10,b}(s)=\frac12\left\{\bm s_b^\prime(\mathcal J_b+W_{bs}^{-1})^{-1}\bm s_b-\log|I_{q_b}+W_{bs}\mathcal J_b|\right\}.\] The first term rewards evidence away from the null and the determinant is the prior-volume penalty.

The production sensitivity averages equal prior mass over \(s\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.0\}\), measured as the standard deviation of one latent cell-type effect in path-ILR units. If the tested cell-type design contains \(L_b-1\) reference contrasts, independent latent effects with variance \(s^2\) induce the reference-invariant covariance \(W_{bs}=s^2\{(I_{L_b-1}+\bm1\bm1^\prime)\otimes I_{d_b}\}\in\mathbb S_{++}^{q_b}\). The reported mixture is \(\log\mathrm{BF}^{\rm mix}_{10,b}=\operatorname{logsumexp}_s\{\log\mathrm{BF}_{10,b}(s)\}-\log4\). This avoids changing the evidence when a different reference cell type is chosen. It does not repair misspecification of the estimated-path Gaussian model, so both the LRT p-values and Bayes-factor thresholds are subjected to the same within-mouse label permutations and restricted wild-cluster nulls as the Wald ablation.

On the full mouse analysis, the Gaussian path model was eligible for 4,472 of 4,825 blocks and made 200 analytic BH discoveries, 4.47\% of eligible blocks. The score-mixture Bayes factor exceeded 10 for 277 blocks and 100 for 163, while the BIC Bayes factor exceeded those thresholds for 83 and 51. On 4,232 blocks also eligible for the isoform-space EC GLMM, the EC model selected 305 and the Gaussian path LRT selected 195, with 105 shared calls and discovery-set Jaccard index 0.266. This is much less discrepant than the estimate-once sandwich Wald analysis, but nominal thresholds still require the structured-null check below.

All 96 mouse shards completed the 32 within-mouse label permutations. Their pooled LRT rejection rates were 4.16\%, 1.51\%, and 0.478\% at nominal 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, and one null family produced a mean 11.0 analytic BH calls. Thus the center is conservative but the deep tail is inflated. The restricted wild-cluster sensitivity covered 92 shards and gave pooled rates 2.24\%, 0.638\%, and 0.150\%, with a mean 1.56 BH calls. Directly estimating the expected false-discovery count from each complete label-permutation family retained 12 LRT calls at empirical FDR 0.05, compared with 194 under the wild null; the conservative intersection therefore contains 12. The corresponding counts were 12 and 269 for the score-mixture Bayes factor and 27 and 99 for the BIC approximation. At the fixed threshold \(\mathrm{BF}\geq10\), label permutations predict 28.3 null score-mixture calls among 4,472 tests versus 277 observed, and 7.94 null BIC calls versus 83 observed. Bayes factors therefore rank a small supported tail, especially after the BIC dimension penalty, but neither the score approximation nor the LRT supplies calibrated analytic thresholds. The production EC GLMM remains the primary inferential model; estimate-once Gaussian results are an ablation or must use design-matched empirical calibration.

Across 963 mouse block tests and 725 genes shared between both subject halves and the EC GLMM, the isoform-space model had 35 genes passing BH in both halves, held-half nominal replication 0.668, and fold log-p correlation 0.432. The Gaussian path LRT had 17 replicated genes, held-half replication 0.724, and fold correlation 0.716. Among 14,014 matched cell-type-pair by path coordinates, overall sign agreement was 0.573 and effect correlation was 0.148. Requiring \(|z|\geq1.96\) in both halves retained 171 coordinates from 54 blocks, with sign agreement 0.947 and correlation 0.932. The strong effects are more directionally stable than those selected by the sandwich Wald statistic, although this does not establish tail calibration.

The dense mouse run used 4.46 aggregate worker hours. Woodbury used 4.83 hours and therefore did not improve aggregate mouse time, although it reduced the slowest shard from 502 to 418 seconds. On an identical 20-block high-dimensional human estimate set, Woodbury reduced runtime from 193.6 to 168.0 seconds, 13.2\%, and changed the largest LRT statistic by only \(2.2\times10^{-7}\). The Woodbury implementation is retained because its memory and factorization dimension do not grow cubically with the number of pseudobulks per mouse.

An evenly spaced human sensitivity used 11 of 96 shards, with 935 eligible blocks among 1,329 candidates. The analytic LRT made 153 BH calls, the score-mixture Bayes factor exceeded 10 for 196 blocks, and the BIC Bayes factor exceeded 10 for 19. Across eight within-subject label-permutation families, LRT rejection was 6.08\%, 3.37\%, and 2.09\% at nominal 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001. The first threshold is near nominal but the extreme tail remains inflated. Empirical tail-FDR estimates retained 37 LRT, 32 score-mixture, and 19 BIC-evidence calls at 0.05, but eight null families give a coarse sensitivity rather than a definitive genome-wide calibration.

The genome-wide hierarchical model supplies \(\bm\theta_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\). Let \(s_b(t)\in\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\) map isoform \(t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\) to its path. The baseline spliced-isoform mass is the scalar \(\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}} \theta_{bit}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+\). For \(t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\), local refinement uses \[
 \theta_{bit}(\bm\delta_{bi})
 =\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}\frac{\theta_{bit}^{(0)}
 \exp\{(Q_b)_{s_b(t),\cdot}\bm\delta_{bi}\}}
 {\sum_{u\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}}\theta_{biu}^{(0)}
 \exp\{(Q_b)_{s_b(u),\cdot}\bm\delta_{bi}\}},
\]
where \((Q_b)_{s,\cdot}\in\mathbb R^{1\times d_b}\). Nuisance-isoform abundances are unchanged. This preserves total spliced mass and the relative isoform mixture within each path while estimating only \(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\).

\subsection*{Path response and inferential covariance}

Each column of \(C_b\in\{0,1\}^{S_b\times T_g}\) corresponding to \(t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\) contains one 1 in row \(s_b(t)\); nuisance isoform columns are zero. Define \[
 \bm a_{bi}=C_b\bm\theta_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b},\qquad
 \bm\psi_{bi}=\frac{\bm a_{bi}}
 {\bm1_{S_b}^\prime\bm a_{bi}}\in\Delta^{S_b-1},\qquad
 \bm y_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}.
\]
The perturbation parameterization implies \(\bm y_{bi}=\bm y_{bi}^{(0)}+\bm\delta_{bi}\), where \(\bm y_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) is computed from the baseline.

Let \(I_{\delta,bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) be the expected Fisher information of the summed paired-primer log likelihood with respect to \(\bm\delta_{bi}\). The conditional covariance is \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}=I_{\delta,bi}^{-1}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) when the information is positive definite. It conditions on the fitted total spliced mass, the isoform mixture within each path, and every nuisance-isoform abundance. Its only free directions are the \(d_b\) path perturbations.

\subsection*{Full-isoform nuisance (profile) covariance}

The profile calculation is a covariance sensitivity analysis, not a second estimate of path usage. It retains the same \(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\) obtained from the constrained local fit, but asks how uncertain its path ILRs would be if all \(T_g\) isoform log-abundances were locally free. Equivalently, after a smooth reparameterization into path ILRs and \(T_g-d_b\) nuisance coordinates, it approximates the path-ILR block of the inverse Fisher information after profiling the nuisance coordinates. No unconstrained isoform likelihood is optimized, so \emph{profile} below means this local expected-Fisher approximation rather than an exact profile-likelihood fit.

Set \(\bm\eta_{bi}=\log\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{T_g}\), \(D_{bi}=\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi})\in \mathbb R^{T_g\times T_g}\), \(\bm d_{bp}=A_{bp}^\prime\bm1_{E_{bp}}\in\mathbb R^{T_g}\), and \(\widehat{\bm q}_{bip}=A_{bp}\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi} \in\mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}}\), \(\widehat Z_{bip}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\widehat{\bm q}_{bip} \in\mathbb R_+\), and \(\bm r_{0,bip}=(\bm d_{bp}\odot\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi})/ \widehat Z_{bip} \in\mathbb R^{T_g}\). The primer-specific expected information is \[
 I_{\eta,bip}=N_{bip}\left[
 \frac{D_{bi}A_{bp}^\prime
 \operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\bm q}_{bip}^{-1})A_{bp}D_{bi}}
 {\widehat Z_{bip}}
 -\bm r_{0,bip}\bm r_{0,bip}^\prime
 \right]\in\mathbb S_+^{T_g}.
\]
The paired-primer information is \(I_{\eta,bi}=\sum_{p=1}^P I_{\eta,bip}\in\mathbb S_+^{T_g}\).

The covariance must be propagated through the isoform-to-path map. Let \(\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}} \widehat\theta_{bit}\in\mathbb R_+\) and \(a_{bis}=\sum_{t:s_b(t)=s}\widehat\theta_{bit}\in\mathbb R_+\). Define \(R_{bi}=\partial\log\bm\psi_{bi}/\partial\bm\eta_{bi}^\prime \in\mathbb R^{S_b\times T_g}\) entrywise by \[
 (R_{bi})_{st}=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathbb 1\{s_b(t)=s\}\widehat\theta_{bit}/a_{bis}
  -\widehat\theta_{bit}/\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp},
  &t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp},\\
  0,&t\notin\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \[
 J_{bi}=\frac{\partial\bm y_{bi}}
 {\partial\bm\eta_{bi}^\prime}
 =Q_b^\prime R_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times T_g}.
\]

The multinomial likelihood necessarily has a global-scale null direction, and EC ambiguity can create additional null directions. Write the symmetric eigendecomposition \[
 I_{\eta,bi}=U_{bi}\operatorname{diag}(\bm\lambda_{bi})U_{bi}^\prime,
\]
where \(U_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{T_g\times T_g}\) is orthogonal and \(\bm\lambda_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{T_g}\). An eigenvalue is treated as positive when \(\lambda_{bit}>10^{-8}\max_u|\lambda_{biu}|\). Let \(\mathcal P_{bi}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,T_g\}\) index these positive eigenvalues, let \(\mathcal N_{bi}\) index the remainder, and let \(U_{bi,+}\in\mathbb R^{T_g\times|\mathcal P_{bi}|}\) and \(U_{bi,0}\in\mathbb R^{T_g\times|\mathcal N_{bi}|}\) contain the corresponding eigenvectors.

The path response is identifiable only if its Jacobian annihilates every information-null direction. The implementation requires \[
 \|J_{bi}U_{bi,0}\|_F
 \leq 10^{-9}\max\{\|J_{bi}\|_F,1\}.
\]
If this fails, \(V_{bi}^{\rm prof}\) is set to an infinite matrix and the estimate is excluded from profile testing. Otherwise, \[
 V_{bi}^{\rm prof}
 =J_{bi}U_{bi,+}
 \operatorname{diag}(\bm\lambda_{bi,\mathcal P}^{-1})
 U_{bi,+}^\prime J_{bi}^\prime
 =J_{bi}I_{\eta,bi}^{+}J_{bi}^\prime
 \in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
Unlike \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\), this covariance allows EC-supported changes in within-path isoform mixtures, spliced mass, and nuisance isoforms to absorb information that would otherwise identify a path contrast. It is therefore the more conservative sensitivity calculation when those nuisance directions are weakly identified.

\subsection*{Cell-resolved likelihood}

For cell \(h\) in pseudobulk \(i\), let \(\bm c_{bihp}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}}\), \(N_{bihp}=\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime\bm c_{bihp}\in\mathbb N_0\), and \(\bm\theta_{bih}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\) be its EC counts, total, and baseline. Every cell shares the same pseudobulk perturbation \(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), but its isoform vector \(\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\) is normalized using its own baseline. The objective is the scalar \[
 \ell_{bi}(\bm\delta_{bi})=
 \sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}\sum_{p=1}^P
 \log\operatorname{Multinomial}\left(
 \bm c_{bihp};N_{bihp},
 \frac{A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})}
 {\bm1_{E_{bp}}^\prime A_{bp}\bm\theta_{bih}(\bm\delta_{bi})}
 \right).
\]
Thus cells contribute assignment information but do not become biological replicates. Let \(U_{bih}\in\mathbb R_+\) be the cell's total UMI count and \(g_{bih}\in[0,1]\) its decoded proportion for the block's gene. The scalar weight is \(w_{bih}=U_{bih}g_{bih}\), and the reported pseudobulk composition is \[
 \bm\psi_{bi}=
 \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}w_{bih}\bm\psi_{bih}}
 {\sum_{h=1}^{H_{bi}}w_{bih}}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}.
\]
Cell- and primer-specific Fisher information matrices in \(\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) are summed, inverted, and propagated through this weighted average to obtain \(V_{bi}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\).

\subsection*{Effective counts and compositional regression}

Treating all gene UMIs as known path assignments is overconfident when ECs are ambiguous. For each fitted \(\bm\psi_{bi}\), define the unit-multinomial ILR covariance \[
 G_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\operatorname{diag}(\bm\psi_{bi}^{-1})Q_b
 \in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
The scalar effective count matches \(G_{bi}/n\) to the EC-derived \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\) in Frobenius inner product \(\langle U,W\rangle_F=\operatorname{tr}(U^\prime W)\), for \(U,W\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times d_b}\): \[
 n_{bi}^{\rm eff}=
 \min\left\{N_{bi}^{\rm gene},
 \frac{\langle G_{bi},G_{bi}\rangle_F}
 {\langle G_{bi},V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\rangle_F}\right\}\in\mathbb R_+,
\]
where \(N_{bi}^{\rm gene}\in\mathbb N_0\) is the gene UMI count. Fractional path counts are \(\bm x_{bi}=n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\bm\psi_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{S_b}\), with \(\bm1_{S_b}^\prime\bm x_{bi}=n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\).

For one block, stack \(n=n_b\in\mathbb N\) observations in a design \(X\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\), where \(k\in\mathbb N\), and coefficient matrix \(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\). The mean composition \(\bm\mu_{bi}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\) is defined by reference-path logits \[
 \log\frac{\mu_{bis}}{\mu_{biS_b}}
 =(X\Beta_b)_{is},\qquad s=1,\ldots,d_b.
\]
The generalized Dirichlet--multinomial likelihood, extended to fractional counts through gamma functions, is \[
 \bm x_{bi}\mid n_{bi}^{\rm eff}
 \sim\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}
 \left(n_{bi}^{\rm eff},\kappa_b\bm\mu_{bi}\right),
\]
where \(\kappa_b\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) is a block-level concentration. Larger \(\kappa_b\) means less overdispersion; the endpoint \(\kappa_b=\infty\) is exactly multinomial.

For nested designs \(X_0\in\mathbb R^{n\times k_0}\) and \(X_1\in\mathbb R^{n\times k_1}\), with \(k_1\geq k_0\) and the first \(k_0,k_1\in\mathbb N\), and the first \(k_0\) columns of \(X_1\) equal to \(X_0\), let \(\widehat\ell_0,\widehat\ell_1\in\mathbb R\) be maximized scalar log likelihoods. The statistic and nominal degrees of freedom are \[
 \Lambda_b=2(\widehat\ell_1-\widehat\ell_0)\in\mathbb R_+,
 \qquad \nu_b=(k_1-k_0)d_b\in\mathbb N_0.
\]
The fixed-concentration analysis estimates \(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) under \(X_0\) and uses it under \(X_1\). The free-concentration sensitivity estimates \(\widehat\kappa_{1b}\in\mathbb R_+\cup\{\infty\}\) under \(X_1\). In the latter case both models contain one concentration nuisance parameter, so \(\nu_b\) is unchanged. Both finite and exact multinomial endpoints are evaluated.

\subsection*{Gaussian mouse-replicate regression}

For a condition test within one block and cell type, stack \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) over \(n\in\mathbb N\) mice. Let \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\), \(k\in\mathbb N\), contain an intercept and condition indicators, \(\Beta_b^{\rm GLS}\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\) be regression coefficients, and \(\bm\epsilon_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) be residuals: \[
 \widehat{\bm y}_{bi}=(X_{b,i\cdot}\Beta_b^{\rm GLS})^\prime
 +\bm\epsilon_{bi},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\bm\epsilon_{bi})=
 V_{bi}+\tau_b^2I_{d_b}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
Here \(X_{b,i\cdot}\in\mathbb R^{1\times k}\), and \(\tau_b^2\in\mathbb R_+\) is residual between-mouse variance estimated by REML under the null. If \(r_b\in\mathbb N\) non-reference condition columns are tested, their coefficients form \(\bm\beta_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb R^{r_bd_b}\) with covariance \(\Sigma_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb S_+^{r_bd_b}\). The Wald statistic is \[
 W_b=(\bm\beta_b^{\rm test})^\prime
 (\Sigma_b^{\rm test})^{-1}\bm\beta_b^{\rm test}\in\mathbb R_+,
 \qquad \nu_b^{\rm GLS}=r_bd_b.
\]

\subsection*{Paired cell-type test and permutations}

For a cell-type test, let \(M_b,L_b\in\mathbb N\) be the numbers of represented biological mice and cell types, respectively. The null design \(X_{0b}\in\{0,1\}^{n_b\times M_b}\) contains an intercept-coded set of mouse fixed effects. The alternative \(X_{1b}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) appends treatment-coded cell-type indicators. Consequently \(k_0=M_b\), \(k_1=M_b+L_b-1\), and \(\nu_b=(L_b-1)d_b\). The tested coefficient block is \(\Beta_b^{\rm cell}\in\mathbb R^{(L_b-1)\times d_b}\), and \[
 H_{0b}:\Beta_b^{\rm cell}=0
 \quad\text{versus}\quad
 H_{1b}:\Beta_b^{\rm cell}\neq0.
\]
Mouse effects absorb condition and every shift shared by cell types from the same mouse.

For \(R=500\in\mathbb N\) permutations and \(r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}\), cell-type labels are shuffled only among pseudobulks from the same mouse, producing \(X_{1b}^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\) and statistic \(\Lambda_b^{(r)}\in\mathbb R_+\). Let \(\varepsilon_b=10^{-6}\max(1,|\Lambda_b|)\in\mathbb R_+\). The finite-permutation p-value is \[
 p_b=\frac{1+\sum_{r=1}^{R}
 \mathbb{1}\{\Lambda_b^{(r)}\geq\Lambda_b-\varepsilon_b\}}{R+1}\in(0,1],
 \qquad R=500.
\]
The null coefficients and \(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\) are reused, but each permuted alternative is optimized. The tolerance counts numerically equal likelihood optima as ties. Benjamini--Hochberg correction is applied across canonical partitions with at least 30 pseudobulks.

\subsection*{Joint condition tests}

ACAT combines \(J\in\mathbb N\) component p-values \(p_j\in(0,1)\), \(j=1,\ldots,J\), into the scalar \[
 p_{\rm ACAT}=\frac12-\frac1\pi\arctan\left[
 \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\tan\{\pi(1/2-p_j)\}\right]\in(0,1).
\]
One level combines cell-type-specific p-values for a path partition; a second combines distinct partitions within a gene.

The shared-effect Gaussian model for block \(b\) uses \(M\in\mathbb N\) mice indexed by \(m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), and \(L_m\in\mathbb N\) cell types for mouse \(m\), indexed by \(c\in\{1,\ldots,L_m\}\). Its response is \(\bm y_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), design row \(X_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{1\times k}\), where \(k\in\mathbb N\), coefficient matrix \(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\), mouse random effect \(\bm u_{bm}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), and residual \(\bm e_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\): \[
 \bm y_{bmc}=(X_{bmc}\Beta_b)^\prime+\bm u_{bm}+\bm e_{bmc}.
\]
The random terms satisfy \(\operatorname E(\bm u_{bm})=\operatorname E(\bm e_{bmc}) =\bm0_{d_b}\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\bm u_{bm})=\tau_{\rm mouse}^2I_{d_b}\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(\bm e_{bmc})= V_{bmc}+\tau_{\rm resid}^2I_{d_b}\). They are independent across mice, and residuals are independent across cell types conditional on \(\bm u_{bm}\). Hence, for \(c\neq c'\), \[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\bm y_{bmc},\bm y_{bmc'})=
 \tau_{\rm mouse}^2I_{d_b},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\bm y_{bmc})=
 V_{bmc}+(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2+\tau_{\rm resid}^2)I_{d_b},
\]
where \(V_{bmc}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\), and \(\tau_{\rm mouse}^2,\tau_{\rm resid}^2\in\mathbb R_+\) are shared-mouse and residual variance components estimated by two-component REML under the no-condition model. Condition labels are permuted once per mouse and applied to all its cell types.

\subsection*{Multinomial-logit GLMM}

The formal generalized linear mixed model uses the same shared-condition design as the joint Gaussian model but retains the multinomial response. Let \(\widetilde{\bm x}_{bmc}\in\mathbb N_0^{S_b}\) be integer path counts with \(\widetilde n_{bmc}=\bm1_{S_b}^\prime\widetilde{\bm x}_{bmc} \in\mathbb N\). They are obtained from the fractional effective counts \(\bm x_{bmc}\) by rounding \(n_{bmc}^{\rm eff}\) to the nearest positive integer and distributing the remaining molecules to paths by largest fractional remainder. This makes the conditional response a proper multinomial while changing each effective total by at most one.

For design row \(X_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{1\times k}\), fixed coefficients \(\Beta_b\in\mathbb R^{k\times d_b}\), and mouse random intercept \(\bm u_{bm}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\), define \[
 \bm\xi_{bmc}=X_{bmc}\Beta_b+\bm u_{bm}^\prime
 \in\mathbb R^{1\times d_b},
\qquad
 \bm\mu_{bmc}=
 \operatorname{softmax}\{(\bm\xi_{bmc},0)\}
 \in\Delta^{S_b-1}.
\]
The final path is the reference category. The model is \[
 \widetilde{\bm x}_{bmc}\mid\bm u_{bm}
 \sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(\widetilde n_{bmc},\bm\mu_{bmc}),
 \qquad
 \bm u_{bm}\sim N(\bm0_{d_b},\sigma_b^2I_{d_b}),
\]
where \(\sigma_b^2\in\mathbb R_+\) is re-estimated under both null and alternative. An isotropic random-effect covariance is used because some blocks have few mice; an unstructured \(d_b\times d_b\) covariance would introduce \(d_b(d_b+1)/2\) variance parameters per block.

Let \(\ell_{bm}(\bm u;\Beta_b,\sigma_b)\in\mathbb R\) be mouse \(m\)'s conditional multinomial log likelihood plus its Gaussian log density, and let \[
 \widehat{\bm u}_{bm}
 =\arg\max_{\bm u\in\mathbb R^{d_b}}\ell_{bm}(\bm u;\Beta_b,\sigma_b),
 \qquad
 H_{bm}=-\frac{\partial^2\ell_{bm}}
 {\partial\bm u\,\partial\bm u^\prime}
 \bigg|_{\widehat{\bm u}_{bm}}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
The Laplace log marginal likelihood, up to constants common to nested models, is \[
 \ell_b^{\rm Lap}(\Beta_b,\sigma_b)
 =\sum_{m=1}^M\left[
   \ell_{bm}(\widehat{\bm u}_{bm};\Beta_b,\sigma_b)
   +\frac{d_b}{2}\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log|H_{bm}|
 \right]\in\mathbb R.
\]
The implementation differentiates this approximation through 30 damped Newton updates for every \(\widehat{\bm u}_{bm}\). The null contains cell-type intercepts; the alternative appends \(r_b\) condition columns. The likelihood-ratio statistic has nominal degrees of freedom \(r_bd_b\).

\subsection*{Additional EC likelihoods and inference approximations}

The path-count GLMM above treats estimated effective path counts as its response. The following restatement of the observed-EC model supplies context for the additional Dirichlet--multinomial, Laplace, CAVI, Monte Carlo, and R\'enyi inference comparisons. For gene \(g\), let \(T_g\in\mathbb N\) be its number of supported isoforms, \(d_g=T_g-1\), \(I_g\in\mathbb N\) the number of retained pseudobulks, \(M_g\in\mathbb N\) the number of mice, and \(P=2\) the primer count. Primer \(p\) has \(K_{gp}\in\mathbb N\) gene-assigned ECs. Define the observed counts \(\bm c_{gip}\in\mathbb N_0^{K_{gp}}\), total \(n_{gip}=\bm1_{K_{gp}}^\prime\bm c_{gip}\in\mathbb N_0\), and fixed compatibility or conditional-weight matrix \(A_{gp}\in\mathbb R_+^{K_{gp}\times T_g}\). Unsupported annotated isoforms and ECs not uniquely assigned to \(g\) are excluded.

Let \(X_g\in\mathbb R^{I_g\times q}\) be the fixed-effect design, \(B_g\in\mathbb R^{q\times d_g}\) its coefficient matrix, and \(u_{gm}\in\mathbb R^{d_g}\) mouse \(m\)'s random isoform-logit intercept. With mouse index \(m(i)\in\{1,\ldots,M_g\}\), the reference-isoform parameterization is \[
 \eta_{gi}=\{X_{gi}B_g+u_{g,m(i)}^\prime,0\}
 \in\mathbb R^{T_g},
 \qquad
 u_{gm}\sim N(0,\sigma_g^2I_{d_g}).
\]
The EC probability vector is \(\bm q_{gip}(\bm\eta_{gi})\) defined at the start of this appendix. Thus the two primers share \(B_g\) and \(u_{gm}\), but retain distinct EC maps and positional-coverage weights. The conditional model is either \[
 \bm c_{gip}\mid u_{g,m(i)}
 \sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n_{gip},\bm q_{gip})
\]
or \[
 \bm c_{gip}\mid u_{g,m(i)}
 \sim\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}
 (n_{gip},\kappa_g\bm q_{gip}),
 \qquad \kappa_g\in\mathbb R_+.
\]
The latter adds pseudobulk-level EC overdispersion. It is a direct Dirichlet--multinomial over EC categories, not a Dirichlet model over latent isoform allocations.

An alternative overdispersion model retains the multinomial observation but adds an isotropic observation-level logistic-normal effect. For pseudobulk $i$, let $e_{gi}\in\mathbb R^{d_g}$ and replace the linear predictor by \[
 \eta_{gi}=\{X_{gi}B_g+u_{g,m(i)}^\prime+e_{gi}^\prime,0\},
 \qquad e_{gi}\sim N(0,\tau_g^2I_{d_g}),
 \quad \tau_g\in\mathbb R_+.
\]
The same $e_{gi}$ enters both primer likelihoods, because it perturbs the underlying isoform composition rather than each primer's EC probabilities independently. Consequently this model allows correlated extra-multinomial variation across the two primer halves. It is a logistic-normal analogue of the Dirichlet--multinomial, not an identical distribution: its dispersion is isotropic in reference-logit coordinates, whereas the Dirichlet--multinomial has one concentration on the EC simplex.

Laplace inference maximizes the joint conditional density over \(u_g\in\mathbb R^{M_g\times d_g}\). Its negative Hessian satisfies \(H_g\in\mathbb S_+^{M_gd_g}\), and the marginal approximation adds the correction \(-\tfrac12\log|H_g|\). The outer fit optimizes \(B_g\), \(\sigma_g\), and, for the overdispersed model, \(\kappa_g\).

Let \(\bm\theta_g\in\mathbb R^{p_g}\) be the outer parameter vector and \(\widehat{\bm u}_{gm}\in\mathbb R^{d_g}\) the cluster mode. The mode score is \(\bm s_{gm}=\partial\ell_{gm}/\partial\bm u_{gm}\in\mathbb R^{d_g}\), and the positive mode Hessian is \(H_{gm}=-\partial\bm s_{gm}/\partial\bm u_{gm}\in\mathbb S_{++}^{d_g}\). The fitted mode satisfies \(\bm s_{gm}(\widehat{\bm u}_{gm},\bm\theta_g)=\bm0_{d_g}\). Rather than retaining and differentiating through every Newton update, the optimized implementation differentiates this stationarity equation implicitly, \[\frac{\partial\widehat{\bm u}_{gm}}{\partial\bm\theta_g}=H_{gm}^{-1}\frac{\partial\bm s_{gm}}{\partial\bm\theta_g}\in\mathbb R^{d_g\times p_g}.\] For a reverse-mode cotangent \(\bm v_{gm}\in\mathbb R^{d_g}\), it solves one \(d_g\times d_g\) system with \(H_{gm}\) and applies the score vector--Jacobian product. This gives the derivative of the same Laplace objective at a converged mode without backpropagating through the 12 Newton updates. Fits that fail the joint outer-gradient and mode-score criteria receive a 30-update continuation. Compiled objectives are reused only among contiguous tests with the same gene, pseudobulk set, and tensor shape; counts and block-specific fixed-effect tensors are dynamic arguments, and the cache is released before the next group.

\subsubsection*{Nonbootstrap Laplace inference}

The block test uses the same fixed-effect tensors as the primary variational fit. Write the null tensor as \(Z_{0b}\in\mathbb R^{I_g\times d_g\times p_{0b}}\) and the alternative as \(Z_{1b}\in\mathbb R^{I_g\times d_g\times p_{1b}}\), where the first \(p_{0b}\) slices of \(Z_{1b}\) equal \(Z_{0b}\). The remaining \(q_b=p_{1b}-p_{0b}=r_bd_b^{\rm path}\) coefficients are exactly the tested block-path interactions. For coefficient vector \(\bm\beta_b\in\mathbb R^{p_{jb}}\), the fixed nonreference-isoform logits for pseudobulk \(i\) are \[
 \bm\eta^{\rm fix}_{gbi}
 =Z_{jb,i,\cdot,\cdot}\bm\beta_b\in\mathbb R^{d_g},
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Thus the likelihood still contains every supported gene isoform and EC; only the tested fixed-effect subspace is block specific.

Let \(\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{0b}\) and \(\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{1b}\) be the maximized Laplace marginal log likelihoods, including re-estimation of all shared variance parameters. The nonbootstrap likelihood-ratio statistic and nominal reference distribution are \[
 D_b=2\{\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{1b}
          -\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{0b}\},
 \qquad D_b\mathrel{\dot\sim}\chi^2_{q_b}.
\]
The alternative starts from the null estimate with its \(q_b\) added coefficients set to zero. A BIC approximation to the alternative-versus-null Bayes factor uses the number \(M_g\) of independent mice rather than the number of pseudobulks: \[
 \log \widehat{\mathrm{BF}}_{10,b}^{\rm BIC}
 =\frac12\{D_b-q_b\log M_g\}\in\mathbb R.
\]
This is not a prior-integrated Bayes factor. Both calculations require only the null and alternative fits, compared with one null and one alternative fit for every parametric-bootstrap replicate. Their calibration can fail when the number of mice is small, a variance estimate lies on its boundary, or the Laplace outer optimizer is inaccurate. They are therefore compared with the completed mouse bootstrap analysis before being used as a replacement for empirical calibration.

For variational inference, use the diagonal Gaussian family \[
 Q(u_g)=\prod_{m=1}^{M_g}\prod_{j=1}^{d_g}
 N(u_{gmj};\mu_{gmj},s_{gmj}^2),
 \quad \mu_g,s_g\in\mathbb R^{M_g\times d_g},\quad s_{gmj}>0.
\]
For the multinomial likelihood, latent EC-to-isoform responsibilities \(r_{gipkt}\in[0,1]\), with \(\sum_t r_{gipkt}=1\) on the support of \(A_{gpkt}\), give the optimized lower bound \[
 E_Q\!\left[\log\sum_t A_{gpkt}e^{\eta_{git}}\right]
 \geq
 \log\sum_t A_{gpkt}e^{E_Q\eta_{git}}.
\]
The normalizer needs an upper bound. For one observation and primer, let \(m_{gipt}=E_Q\eta_{git}+\log\sum_kA_{gpkt}\), \(v_{gipt}=\operatorname{Var}_Q(\eta_{git})\), and \(a_{gip}\in\Delta^{T_g-1}\). The tilted softmax bound of Knowles and Minka is \[
 E_Q\log\sum_{t=1}^{T_g}e^{\eta_{git}+\log\sum_kA_{gpkt}}
 \leq \frac12\sum_ta_{gipt}^2v_{gipt}
 +\log\sum_t\exp\!\left\{
 m_{gipt}+\frac{1-2a_{gipt}}2v_{gipt}\right\},
\]
where the local coordinate satisfies \[
 a_{gip}=\operatorname{softmax}\{m_{gip}
 +\tfrac12(1-2a_{gip})\mathbin\odot v_{gip}\}.
\]
Alternating the analytic responsibilities, this local fixed point, and the global Gaussian parameters maximizes a tractable lower bound on the multinomial evidence. The cited method is non-conjugate variational message passing rather than conjugate CAVI; here ``tilted CAVI'' refers only to this block-coordinate implementation.

The full-covariance extension factorizes only across mice. If mouse $m$ has $q_{gm}\in\mathbb N$ observed pseudobulks, define its complete latent block \[
 z_{gm}=(u_{gm}^\prime,e_{gi_1}^\prime,\ldots,e_{gi_{q_{gm}}}^\prime)^\prime
 \in\mathbb R^{L_{gm}},
 \qquad L_{gm}=(q_{gm}+1)d_g,
\]
where the $e$ components are omitted in the model without observation noise. Its prior covariance is diagonal, \[
 P_{gm}=\operatorname{diag}
 (\sigma_g^2 I_{d_g},\tau_g^2 I_{q_{gm}d_g})
 \in\mathbb S_+^{L_{gm}},
\]
but its posterior approximation is unrestricted within mouse, \[
 Q(z_g)=\prod_{m=1}^{M_g}N(z_{gm};\mu_{gm},\Sigma_{gm}),
 \quad \mu_{gm}\in\mathbb R^{L_{gm}},\quad
 \Sigma_{gm}\in\mathbb S_{++}^{L_{gm}}.
\]
Thus full covariance describes posterior dependence among isoform logits and between a shared mouse effect and its pseudobulk residuals; it does not add an unstructured covariance parameter to the generative model.

For a block-specific cell-type test, the EC likelihood still contains all \(T_g\) supported isoforms of gene \(g\); the response is not reduced to path counts. Let \(C_b^{\rm cell}\in\mathbb N\) be the number of represented cell types, \(Z_b\in\mathbb R^{I_g\times q_0}\) the one-hot condition design, and \(W_b\in\mathbb R^{I_g\times(C_b^{\rm cell}-1)}\) the treatment-coded cell-type design. The pooled-baseline path Jacobian \(J_b\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times d_g}\), its right inverse \(H_b=J_b^\prime(J_bJ_b^\prime)^{-1}\in\mathbb R^{d_g\times d_b}\), and the kernel basis \(N_b\in\mathbb R^{d_g\times(d_g-d_b)}\) are defined in the main methods.
The free-logit predictor is \[
 \eta_{gi,-}=Z_{bi}B_{g0}
 +\sum_{j=1}^{C_b^{\rm cell}-1}W_{bij}
   (N_b\bm\alpha_{bj}+H_b\bm\gamma_{bj})
 +u_{g,m(i)}+e_{gi}
 \in\mathbb R^{d_g},
\]
where \(B_{g0}\in\mathbb R^{q_0\times d_g}\) contains unrestricted condition effects, \(\bm\alpha_{bj}\in\mathbb R^{d_g-d_b}\) contains cell-type nuisance effects tangent to constant path usage, and \(\bm\gamma_{bj}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) is cell type \(j\)'s block-path contrast. The \(e_{gi}\) term is present only in the logistic-normal sensitivity model. The null estimates every \(\bm\alpha_{bj}\) but sets all \(\bm\gamma_{bj}=\bm0_{d_b}\); the alternative has \((C_b^{\rm cell}-1)d_b\) additional coefficients. The combined columns of \(N_b\) and \(H_b\) span all \(d_g\) isoform-logit directions, so the alternative permits unrestricted cell-type isoform composition while the null removes the local path derivative at the pooled baseline. This construction makes the EC GLMM a per-block test while preserving gene-wide ambiguity and nuisance mass.

For pseudobulk \(i\), let \(R_{gi}\in\{0,1\}^{d_g\times L_{g,m(i)}}\) select and sum its mouse and pseudobulk effects. Its free-logit covariance is $R_{gi}\Sigma_{g,m(i)}R_{gi}^\prime\in\mathbb S_+^{d_g}$; append a zero row and column for the reference isoform to obtain $V_{gi}\in\mathbb S_+^{T_g}$. The correlated tilted bound replaces the variance vector in the diagonal expression. For $m\in\mathbb R^{T_g}$, $V\in\mathbb S_+^{T_g}$, and $a\in\Delta^{T_g-1}$, \[
 E\log\sum_t e^{\eta_t}
 \leq \frac12a^\prime Va
 +\log\sum_t\exp\{m_t+\tfrac12V_{tt}-(Va)_t\},
 \qquad
 a=\operatorname{softmax}\{m+\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(V)-Va\}.
\]
The Gaussian KL term is analytic: \[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_m\Vert P_m)=\frac12\left[
 \operatorname{tr}(P_m^{-1}\Sigma_m)
 +\mu_m^\prime P_m^{-1}\mu_m-L_{gm}
 +\log|P_m|-\log|\Sigma_m|\right].
\]
Each positive-definite $\Sigma_m$ is represented by an unconstrained lower Cholesky factor with exponentiated diagonal. The full fit warm-starts from a diagonal variational posterior by copying its means and marginal variances and setting new off-diagonal entries to zero.

The direct EC Dirichlet--multinomial contains \(\log\Gamma\{\kappa_gq_{gipk}(u)+c_{gipk}\}\), so the tilted softmax bound does not yield coordinate updates for it. Both likelihood families are therefore also fit with reparameterized Monte Carlo VI. If \(w(u)=p(c,u)/Q(u)\), the optimized objective is the ELBO \[
 \mathcal L_1=E_Q\log w(u)
\]
or the variational R\'enyi bound of order \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \[
 \mathcal L_\alpha=
 \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log E_Q\{w(u)^{1-\alpha}\}.
\]
The comparison uses \(\alpha=0.5\), antithetic Gaussian draws, and the same diagonal or full Gaussian family. Conditional on \(B_g,\sigma_g,\kappa_g\), both the likelihood and \(Q\) factor by mouse. Writing the corresponding ratio as \(w_m(u_{gm})\), the implementation evaluates the equivalent expression \[
 \mathcal L_\alpha=
 \sum_{m=1}^{M_g}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
 \log E_{Q_m}\{w_m(u_{gm})^{1-\alpha}\}.
\]
This avoids the exponential importance-weight degeneracy caused by forming one product weight over all mice. R\'enyi VI is not called CAVI because its importance-weighted objective has no coordinate update under this model. For the full posterior with observation-level noise, directly optimizing a fixed set of Monte Carlo draws is ill-conditioned: the Cholesky factor can overfit sample correlations when $L_{gm}$ is not small relative to the draw count. This variant is therefore fit with the analytic tilted objective; fresh antithetic draws evaluate its exact-likelihood ELBO and R\'enyi bound.

The block statistic is twice the optimized alternative-minus-null variational objective. Its reference distribution is empirical rather than \(\chi^2\), because it compares optimized evidence bounds and can inherit approximation error. A synchronized label permutation is invalid here because the block null permits real cell-type nuisance effects tangent to constant path usage. Instead, a parametric bootstrap simulates mouse effects and optional pseudobulk effects from the fitted block null, generates paired-primer EC counts with the observed primer-specific pseudobulk totals, and refits both nested models. Each observed statistic is compared with a leave-block-out pool of bootstrap statistics matched by likelihood family and tested dimension \((C_b^{\rm cell}-1)d_b\). Tested dimensions represented by fewer than 20 blocks are combined into a rare-dimension stratum within the same likelihood. Matching gene-depth quartiles fragmented the empirical tail enough to limit BH resolution in the genome-wide analysis. Depth is therefore an independent calibration audit rather than a pooling variable. With null pool \(\mathcal N_{-b}\), \[
 p_b=\frac{1+\sum_{z\in\mathcal N_{-b}}
                   \mathbf1\{z\geq T_b\}}
              {1+|\mathcal N_{-b}|}.
\]
Every bootstrap statistic is calibrated by the same leave-block-out rule. Benjamini--Hochberg values are reported for a likelihood only if these calibrated null \(p\)-values reject between 2.5\% and 7.5\% at level 0.05, both in aggregate and separately within each gene-depth quartile.

\subsection*{Liang--Zeger multinomial GEE}

GEE specifies only the marginal mean and first two moments. It can therefore use the fractional effective counts directly. Define \(\bm p_{bmc}=\bm x_{bmc}/n_{bmc}^{\rm eff}\in\Delta^{S_b-1}\), and let \(\bm p_{bmc,-}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) omit the reference path. Its marginal mean uses the same reference-logit regression, \[
 \bm\mu_{bmc}=\operatorname{softmax}\{(X_{bmc}\Beta_b,0)\},
 \qquad
 G_{bmc}=\operatorname{diag}(\bm\mu_{bmc,-})
 -\bm\mu_{bmc,-}\bm\mu_{bmc,-}^\prime
 \in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}.
\]
The working covariance of one proportion vector is \(A_{bmc}=G_{bmc}/n_{bmc}^{\rm eff}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\). Let \(L_{bmc}\in\mathbb R^{d_b\times d_b}\) be its symmetric square root, and let \[
 D_{bmc}=\frac{\partial\bm\mu_{bmc,-}}
 {\partial\operatorname{vec}(\Beta_b)^\prime}
 =[X_{bmc,1}G_{bmc}\ \cdots\ X_{bmc,k}G_{bmc}]
 \in\mathbb R^{d_b\times kd_b}.
\]

For mouse \(m\), stack its \(q_m\in\mathbb N\) observed cell types into \(\bm p_{bm,-}\in\mathbb R^{q_md_b}\), mean \(\bm\mu_{bm,-}\in\mathbb R^{q_md_b}\), and derivative \(D_{bm}\in\mathbb R^{q_md_b\times kd_b}\). With \(L_{bm}=\operatorname{blockdiag}_c(L_{bmc})\) and exchangeable \(R_m(\alpha_b)\in\mathbb R^{q_m\times q_m}\), the Liang--Zeger working covariance is \[
 W_{bm}=\phi_bL_{bm}
 \{R_m(\alpha_b)\otimes I_{d_b}\}L_{bm}^\prime
 \in\mathbb S_+^{q_md_b}.
\]
The scale \(\phi_b\in\mathbb R_+\) and working correlation \(\alpha_b\in(-1/(q_{\max}-1),1)\) are updated from whitened Pearson residuals. Coefficients solve \[
 \sum_{m=1}^M D_{bm}^\prime W_{bm}^{-1}
 (\bm p_{bm,-}-\bm\mu_{bm,-})=\bm0_{kd_b}.
\]
If \(\bm U_{bm}=D_{bm}^\prime W_{bm}^{-1} (\bm p_{bm,-}-\bm\mu_{bm,-})\in\mathbb R^{kd_b}\) and \(\mathcal H_b=\sum_mD_{bm}^\prime W_{bm}^{-1}D_{bm} \in\mathbb S_+^{kd_b}\), the canonical cluster-robust covariance is \[
 \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\{\operatorname{vec}(\widehat\Beta_b)\}
 =\mathcal H_b^+
 \left(\sum_{m=1}^M\bm U_{bm}\bm U_{bm}^\prime\right)
 \mathcal H_b^+
 \in\mathbb S_+^{kd_b}.
\]
The condition coefficients are tested by a robust Wald statistic. No small-sample sandwich correction is imposed; synchronized mouse-level permutations assess whether the canonical large-\(M\) reference is adequate.

\subsection*{Matched power benchmark}

Power is evaluated only for partitions represented by exactly two conditions. For replicate \(r\), condition labels are first permuted among mice. Let \(z_{bmc}^{(r)}\in\{0,1\}\) be the resulting non-reference condition indicator and let \(\bm v_b\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\) be a reproducible random unit vector. For ILR effect \(\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5\}\), define \[
 \bm y_{bmc}^{(r,\gamma)}
 =\bm y_{bmc}+\gamma z_{bmc}^{(r)}\bm v_b,\qquad
 \bm\psi_{bmc}^{(r,\gamma)}
 =\operatorname{softmax}\{Q_b\bm y_{bmc}^{(r,\gamma)}\}.
\]
The shifted effective counts are \(n_{bmc}^{\rm eff}\bm\psi_{bmc}^{(r,\gamma)}\). Thus every method sees the same imposed ILR effect, observed effective depth, cell-type pattern, and mouse clustering. For each simulated alternative, null-calibrated power uses the finite-permutation rank of its statistic among synchronized null statistics for the same block and method. This event-matched calibration preserves dimension, depth, and cluster structure.

\section{Supporting Algorithms}

All algorithms operate independently over canonical blocks unless a joint gene-level combination is stated. ``Fit'' below means bounded L-BFGS with analytic gradients and a fourfold iteration-limit retry after a warm start from the first endpoint.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Construct splice blocks and path maps}
\label{alg:blocks}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require \(K_g\) annotated spliced isoforms with exon chains; retained set \(\mathcal I_g\), \(|\mathcal I_g|=T_g\)
\Ensure Blocks \(b\), maps \(C_b\in\{0,1\}^{S_b\times T_g}\), and bases \(Q_b\in\mathbb R^{S_b\times d_b}\)
\State Split the gene at every annotated exon boundary into atomic intervals.
\State Merge adjacent intervals exonic in every annotated isoform into constitutive anchors.
\State Form internal blocks between consecutive anchors and terminal blocks using pseudo-anchors.
\ForAll{candidate blocks \(b\)}
  \State Record each annotated isoform's ordered intervening exon chain.
  \State Assign identical chains to one path and restrict to \(\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\subseteq\mathcal I_g\).
  \State Remap represented path labels to \(\{1,\ldots,S_b\}\).
  \If{\(S_b<2\)}
    \State discard \(b\)
  \Else
    \State Set \((C_b)_{s_b(t),t}=1\) for \(t\in\mathcal I_b^{\rm sp}\); leave nuisance columns zero.
    \State Construct the Helmert basis \(Q_b\).
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Canonicalize the unordered isoform partition and retain one representative of equivalent blocks.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Fit one pseudobulk's local path composition}
\label{alg:local-fit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Counts \(\bm c_{bip}\in\mathbb N_0^{E_{bp}}\), designs \(A_{bp}\in\mathbb R_+^{E_{bp}\times T_g}\), baseline \(\bm\theta_{bi}^{(0)}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\), \(C_b\), and \(Q_b\)
\Ensure \(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}\), \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\), \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\), and \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\)
\State Initialize \(\bm\delta_{bi}\gets\bm0_{d_b}\).
\If{\(S_b=2\)}
  \State Evaluate the fixed log-ratio grid and retain its best point.
\EndIf
\Function{Objective}{$\bm\delta$}
  \State Form \(\bm\theta_{bi}(\bm\delta)\), preserving spliced mass and within-path isoform mixtures.
  \State \Return the negative sum of the \(P=2\) multinomial log likelihoods and its analytic gradient.
\EndFunction
\State Minimize \Call{Objective}{$\bm\delta$} by bounded L-BFGS on \([-20,20]^{d_b}\).
\State Set \(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}\gets \bm\theta_{bi}(\widehat{\bm\delta}_{bi})\).
\State Set \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\gets C_b\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}/ (\bm1_{S_b}^\prime C_b\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi})\).
\State Set \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\gets Q_b^\prime\log\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\).
\State Compute \(I_{\delta,bi}\) by summing primer expected information.
\State Apply the eigenvalue/null-space check to \((I_{\delta,bi},I_{d_b})\); if identifiable, set \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}=I_{\delta,bi}^{-1}\), otherwise set it to infinity.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compute full-isoform nuisance (profile) covariance}
\label{alg:profile}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require \(\widehat{\bm\theta}_{bi}\in\mathbb R_+^{T_g}\), \(A_{bp}\), \(N_{bip}\), \(C_b\), \(Q_b\), relative threshold \(r_{\rm eig}=10^{-8}\), and null tolerance \(a_{\rm null}=10^{-9}\)
\Ensure \(V_{bi}^{\rm prof}\in\mathbb S_+^{d_b}\) and an identifiability flag
\For{\(p=1,\ldots,P\)}
  \State Compute \(\widehat{\bm q}_{bip}\), \(\widehat Z_{bip}\), and \(I_{\eta,bip}\in\mathbb S_+^{T_g}\).
\EndFor
\State Set \(I_{\eta,bi}\gets\sum_p I_{\eta,bip}\).
\State Compute path masses \(a_{bis}\), spliced mass \(\omega_{bi}^{\rm sp}\), and \(R_{bi}=\partial\log\bm\psi_{bi}/\partial\bm\eta_{bi}^\prime\).
\State Set \(J_{bi}\gets Q_b^\prime R_{bi}\).
\State Eigendecompose \(I_{\eta,bi}=U_{bi}\operatorname{diag}(\bm\lambda_{bi})U_{bi}^\prime\).
\State Put \(t\) in \(\mathcal P_{bi}\) when \(\lambda_{bit}>r_{\rm eig}\max_u|\lambda_{biu}|\), and put all other indices in \(\mathcal N_{bi}\).
\If{\(\|J_{bi}U_{bi,0}\|_F> a_{\rm null}\max\{\|J_{bi}\|_F,1\}\)}
  \State Set \(V_{bi}^{\rm prof}\gets\infty_{d_b\times d_b}\) and mark the response unidentified.
\Else
  \State Set \(V_{bi}^{\rm prof}\gets J_{bi}U_{bi,+}\operatorname{diag} (\bm\lambda_{bi,\mathcal P}^{-1})U_{bi,+}^\prime J_{bi}^\prime\).
  \State Mark the response identifiable.
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Fit a cell-resolved pseudobulk path composition}
\label{alg:cell-resolved}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Cell EC vectors \(\bm c_{bihp}\), baselines \(\bm\theta_{bih}^{(0)}\), weights \(w_{bih}\), and \(A_{bp},C_b,Q_b\)
\Ensure One pseudobulk response \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\) and covariance \(V_{bi}\)
\State Allocate one shared \(\bm\delta_{bi}\in\mathbb R^{d_b}\).
\ForAll{cells \(h\) and primers \(p\)}
  \State Perturb the cell's baseline, normalize its EC probabilities, and add its multinomial log likelihood and gradient.
\EndFor
\State Optimize the summed objective over \(\bm\delta_{bi}\).
\State Compute each fitted \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bih}\) and their normalized \(w_{bih}\)-weighted mean \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\).
\State Sum cell-by-primer Fisher matrices and propagate their inverse through the weighted composition response to obtain \(V_{bi}\).
\State Set \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}=Q_b^\prime\log \widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Gaussian condition or paired cell-type test}
\label{alg:gaussian}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Responses \(\widehat{\bm y}_{bi}\), covariances \(V_{bi}\), mouse, condition, and cell-type labels; test type
\Ensure One Wald p-value for block \(b\)
\If{testing condition within a cell type}
  \State Retain conditions represented by at least three mice.
  \State Build full design \(X_b\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}\) and reduced design \(X_{0b}\in\mathbb R^{n\times(k-r_b)}\).
\Else
  \State Build mouse-effect design \(X_{0b}\in\{0,1\}^{n_b\times M_b}\).
  \State Append \(L_b-1\) cell-type columns to obtain \(X_{1b}\in\mathbb R^{n_b\times(M_b+L_b-1)}\).
\EndIf
\State Require full column rank in both designs.
\State Estimate \(\tau_b^2\) by REML under the reduced design using \(V_{bi}+\tau_b^2I_{d_b}\).
\State Hold \(\widehat\tau_b^2\) fixed and fit the full design by GLS.
\State Stack the tested coefficients and compute their Wald statistic \(W_b\).
\State Compare \(W_b\) with the applicable chi-squared reference.
\State Calibrate by mouse-level condition permutations or within-mouse cell-type permutations, as appropriate.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Paired compositional cell-type test}
\label{alg:compositional}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require \(\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi}\), \(V_{bi}^{\rm cond}\), mouse and cell-type labels, and \(R=500\)
\Ensure One exact permutation p-value \(p_b\) per canonical block
\State Keep converged, identifiable estimates satisfying the path floor and the three-mouse-per-cell-type requirement.
\State Convert each \((\widehat{\bm\psi}_{bi},V_{bi}^{\rm cond})\) to \(n_{bi}^{\rm eff}\) and \(\bm x_{bi}\).
\State Build \(X_{0b}\) from mouse effects and \(X_{1b}\) by appending cell-type effects.
\State Fit the null coefficients and \(\widehat\kappa_{0b}\).
\State Fit the observed alternative and compute \(\Lambda_b\).
\If{using fixed concentration}
  \State Set \(\kappa_{1b}\gets\widehat\kappa_{0b}\).
\Else
  \State Optimize \(\widehat\kappa_{1b}\) and compare the finite optimum with the exact multinomial endpoint.
\EndIf
\For{\(r=1,\ldots,R\)}
  \State Shuffle cell-type labels within each mouse to form \(X_{1b}^{(r)}\).
  \State Reuse the null fit, refit the alternative, and store \(\Lambda_b^{(r)}\).
\EndFor
\State Compute the tolerance-aware finite-permutation rank \(p_b\).
\State Require \(n_b\geq30\), then apply BH across canonical blocks.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Liang--Zeger multinomial GEE condition test}
\label{alg:gee}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Fractional counts \(\bm x_{bmc}\), design \(X_b\), mouse labels, and tested condition columns
\Ensure Robust Wald statistic and p-value
\State Initialize \(\Beta_b\) from independent multinomial regression and set \(\alpha_b\gets0\), \(\phi_b\gets1\).
\Repeat
  \State Compute \(\bm\mu_{bmc}\), \(G_{bmc}\), \(A_{bmc}\), and \(D_{bmc}\) for every observation.
  \State Whiten residuals by \(A_{bmc}^{-1/2}\).
  \State Update \(\phi_b\) from squared Pearson residuals and \(\alpha_b\) from within-mouse Pearson cross-products.
  \State Construct every \(W_{bm}\), quasi-score \(\bm U_{bm}\), and sensitivity matrix \(\mathcal H_b\).
  \State Update \(\operatorname{vec}(\Beta_b)\gets \operatorname{vec}(\Beta_b)+\mathcal H_b^+\sum_m\bm U_{bm}\).
\Until{the maximum coefficient update is at most \(10^{-8}\)}
\State Form the cluster sandwich covariance \(\mathcal H_b^+(\sum_m\bm U_{bm}\bm U_{bm}^\prime)\mathcal H_b^+\).
\State Compute the robust Wald test for all condition coefficients.
\State Permute condition once per mouse and repeat the complete GEE fit.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Laplace multinomial GLMM condition test}
\label{alg:glmm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Effective counts, nested designs, mouse labels, and random-effect dimension \(d_b\)
\Ensure Likelihood-ratio statistic and p-value
\State Integerize each effective count vector by largest remainder.
\ForAll{null and alternative models}
  \State Initialize fixed coefficients from multinomial regression and initialize \(\sigma_b\).
  \Repeat
    \ForAll{mice \(m\)}
      \State Find \(\widehat{\bm u}_{bm}\) with 30 damped Newton updates.
      \State Compute random-effect Hessian \(H_{bm}\) and its log determinant.
    \EndFor
    \State Sum the Laplace log marginal likelihood and differentiate through the Newton updates.
    \State Update \(\Beta_b\) and \(\log\sigma_b\) by bounded L-BFGS.
  \Until{the outer gradient tolerance or iteration limit is reached}
\EndFor
\State Compute twice the null-to-alternative Laplace log-likelihood gain.
\State Compare with \(\chi^2_{r_bd_b}\).
\State For permutations, reuse the fitted null and refit each permuted alternative.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Nonbootstrap paired-EC Laplace block test}
\label{alg:ec-laplace-lrt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Paired EC counts, compatibility matrices, nested tensors \(Z_{0b},Z_{1b}\), and mouse indices
\Ensure LRT p-value and BIC Bayes-factor approximation
\State Fit the null by maximizing its Laplace marginal log likelihood.
\State Append \(q_b\) zero coefficients to the fitted null parameters.
\State Warm-start and fit the alternative, re-estimating shared variance parameters.
\State Set \(D_b\gets 2\max\{0,\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{1b} -\widehat\ell^{\rm Lap}_{0b}\}\).
\State Set \(p_b\gets\Pr\{\chi^2_{q_b}\geq D_b\}\).
\State Set \(\log\widehat{\mathrm{BF}}_{10,b}^{\rm BIC} \gets(D_b-q_b\log M_g)/2\).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Inference comparison for the paired EC-count GLMM}
\label{alg:ec-glmm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Paired counts \(\bm c_{gip}\), maps \(A_{gp}\), nested fixed-effect designs, mouse indices, likelihood family, and R\'enyi order \(\alpha=0.5\)
\Ensure Null and alternative evidence approximations and coefficient estimates
\ForAll{null and alternative designs}
  \State Fit the conditional posterior mode of \(u_g\in\mathbb R^{M_g\times d_g}\) by damped Newton updates.
  \State Optimize the Laplace marginal likelihood over \(B_g\), \(\log\sigma_g\), and optional \(\log\kappa_g\).
  \If{the likelihood is multinomial}
    \Repeat
      \State Update every latent EC-to-isoform responsibility from \(A_{gpkt}\exp(E_Q\eta_{git})\).
      \State Iterate every tilted coordinate \(a_{gip}\) to its softmax fixed point.
      \State Update \(B_g,\mu_g,\log s_g,\log\sigma_g\) against the bounded ELBO; damp the global update when the bound decreases.
    \Until{the ELBO gradient tolerance or iteration limit is reached}
  \EndIf
  \State Draw fixed antithetic standard-normal samples and reparameterize \(u_g=\mu_g+s_g\mathbin\odot\epsilon\).
  \State Optimize the exact-likelihood Monte Carlo ELBO \(\mathcal L_1\).
  \State Warm-start from the ELBO fit and optimize \(\mathcal L_{0.5}\); report its effective importance-sample size.
\EndFor
\State Compare each method's null-to-alternative gain on its own objective; do not compare raw objective values across approximation families.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Block-specific paired EC-count GLMM cell-type test}
\label{alg:block-count-glmm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Block \(b\), paired gene EC counts \(\bm c_{gip}\), maps \(A_{gp}\), condition design \(Z_b\), cell-type design \(W_b\), mouse indices, and \(R\) parametric-bootstrap replicates
\Ensure Empirically calibrated block p-value
\State Fit the label-independent pooled isoform baseline, construct the path-ILR Jacobian \(J_b\), set \(H_b=J_b^\prime(J_bJ_b^\prime)^{-1}\), and obtain \(N_b\) from \(\ker(J_b)\).
\State Form the null fixed-effect tensor from unrestricted condition-by-isoform coefficients \(Z_b\otimes I_{d_g}\) and cell-type nuisance columns \(W_{bij}(N_b)_{:s}\).
\State Fit the null full-covariance posterior and variance parameters.
\State Append the \((C_b^{\rm cell}-1)d_b\) columns \(W_{bij}(H_b)_{:s}\), warm-start all null parameters, and fit the observed alternative.
\State Set \(T_b\gets2(\mathcal L_{1b}-\mathcal L_{0b})\), using the same variational objective for both nested fits.
\For{\(r=1,\ldots,R\)}
  \State Draw mouse effects and optional pseudobulk effects from the fitted null; compute primer-specific EC probabilities.
  \State Generate multinomial or Dirichlet--multinomial EC counts while preserving every observed primer-specific pseudobulk total.
  \State Refit the null, warm-starting from the observed null, then append the block-effect columns and fit the nested alternative.
\EndFor
\State Pool converged bootstrap statistics by likelihood, tested dimension, and depth stratum, excluding block \(b\)'s replicates.
\State Compute the finite-pool rank p-value and assess the same rank calculation on every null replicate before applying BH.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Matched semi-synthetic power benchmark}
\label{alg:cluster-power}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Two-condition partitions, effect sizes \(\gamma\), and replicate count \(R_{\rm power}\)
\Ensure Asymptotic and null-calibrated power for each method
\ForAll{eligible blocks \(b\)}
  \State Draw and fix a unit ILR direction \(\bm v_b\).
  \For{\(r=1,\ldots,R_{\rm power}\)}
    \State Permute condition labels among mice.
    \ForAll{nonzero effect sizes \(\gamma\)}
      \State Set \(\bm y_{bmc}^{(r,\gamma)} =\bm y_{bmc}+\gamma z_{bmc}^{(r)}\bm v_b\).
      \State Map shifted ILRs to compositions and effective path counts.
      \State Fit GEE, GLMM, clustered GLS, and Dirichlet--multinomial tests.
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State Rank each alternative statistic against null statistics for its block and method, using the finite-permutation correction.
\State Report asymptotic and event-matched permutation rejection at 0.05.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pairwise and dominant-path sensitivity tests}
\label{alg:sensitivities}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Path estimates and conditional covariances from Algorithm~\ref{alg:local-fit}
\Ensure Calibrated sensitivity tables
\ForAll{cell-type pairs}
  \State Retain \(M\) mice containing both types.
  \State Use \(X_0\in\mathbb R^{2M\times M}\) for mouse effects and \(X_1\in\mathbb R^{2M\times(M+1)}\) for mouse plus cell type.
  \State Permute the two labels within mouse and correct across all block-by-pair hypotheses.
\EndFor
\ForAll{blocks \(b\)}
  \State Rank paths by \(\sum_iN_{bi}^{\rm gene}\psi_{bis}\) and select \(\mathcal S_b\), the two largest, without labels.
  \State Construct selector \(E_b\), two-path basis \(Q_b^{(2)}\), and \(\mathcal R_b=(Q_b^{(2)})^\prime E_b^\prime Q_b\).
  \State Set \(V_{bi}^{(2)}=\mathcal R_bV_{bi}\mathcal R_b^\prime\) and renormalize the two retained proportions.
  \State Run Algorithm~\ref{alg:compositional} and record path-floor sensitivity sweeps.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\caption{Joint condition tests}
\label{alg:joint}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Ensure Partition- or gene-level joint p-values

\section{Supporting Implementation Details}

\subsection*{Splice-block construction}

GTF exons are represented by zero-based, half-open intervals. Exon boundaries define atomic intervals; intervals exonic in every annotated spliced isoform are merged into constitutive anchors. Consecutive anchors define a block, and an isoform path is its ordered exon-interval chain between them. Terminal pseudo-anchors include alternative first and last regions. A gene without a shared constitutive region receives one whole-gene block.

Blocks with one path are discarded. Simple two-path blocks include skipped exons, mutually exclusive exons, alternative donors and acceptors, and retained introns. The implementation also supports nested and multi-path blocks. Precursor isoforms remain nuisance isoforms in the EC model but are not assigned to spliced paths.

Different genomic blocks can induce exactly the same partition of annotated isoforms, for example when several exons always occur together. Such blocks have identical statistical responses and must not count as repeated evidence. A canonical partition is the unordered collection of isoform sets assigned to paths. Equivalent blocks are collapsed before FDR or joint testing; all member interval identifiers remain in the output.

\subsection*{Pseudobulks and local fitting}

Cell isoform proportions from the hierarchical decoder are weighted by cell UMI totals and aggregated by mouse, condition, and cell type. Pseudobulks must contain at least 20 cells and \(10^5\) total UMIs. A block is fit when its gene has at least 25 gene-assigned UMIs in at least two qualifying pseudobulks. Only ECs whose compatible isoforms map uniquely to that gene enter the local likelihood.

Bounded L-BFGS optimizes the paired multinomial likelihood, with \(\|\bm\delta_{bi}\|_\infty\leq20\) to prevent floating-point underflow, where \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) is the maximum absolute component. Two-path fits are initialized from a log-ratio grid because the paired-primer normalizers can make the objective non-concave.

For the cell-resolved variant, sparse cell-by-EC matrices are retained and one local isoform matrix is decoded at a time. The optimizer dimension is still only the number of paths minus one. Cell-specific Fisher contributions are accumulated analytically, so the method does not fit one free splicing parameter per cell.

\subsection*{Identifiability and reliability}

Finite Fisher covariance is not sufficient near a simplex boundary. Parametric bootstrap identified two reliability requirements: \[
  \lambda_{\max}(V_{bi})\leq 1/8
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \min_s\widehat\psi_{bis}\geq 0.15.
\]
Thus every tested log-ratio direction has effective information of at least eight and every fitted path has at least 15\% usage. All estimates are written, together with convergence, identifiability, and reliability flags; only reliable estimates enter GLS. Here \(\lambda_{\max}(V_{bi})\in\mathbb R_+\) is the largest eigenvalue of the \(d_b\times d_b\) covariance, and \(\widehat\psi_{bis}\in[0,1]\) is path \(s\)'s fitted proportion.

\section{Supporting Results and Benchmarks}

\subsection*{Condition-test scope}

The primary analysis used the 25-gene-UMI cutoff and blocks with at most eight represented paths. It fit 10,100 blocks and 359,713 pseudobulk-by-block path estimates; 359,711 fits converged. After reliability filtering, there were 829 conditional and 506 profile tests.

These 1,335 fits represent 829 distinct block-by-cell-type hypotheses, covering 527 distinct splice blocks in eight cell types. The profile analysis is available for a subset of the conditional hypotheses. The possible conditions were 5xFAD, 5xFIRE, 5xFIRE PBS, 5xFIRE transplant, FIRE, and WT. For the 829 conditional hypotheses, 275 compared two conditions, 155 compared three, 153 compared four, 130 compared five, and 116 compared all six. The included set varies because every condition in a test must have at least three independent mouse pseudobulks for that block and cell type.

\subsection*{Condition-test results}

No block was significant at 5\% FDR in either covariance analysis. At the unadjusted \(p<0.05\) level:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the conditional analysis identified 24 block-by-cell-type hypotheses, covering 21 distinct blocks;
  \item the profile analysis identified 20 block-by-cell-type hypotheses, covering 17 distinct blocks;
  \item 20 hypotheses were nominally significant in both analyses.
\end{itemize}
The union is 24 nominal block-by-cell-type hypotheses covering 21 blocks, but the corrected number of significant hypotheses and blocks is zero. Thus there is no FDR-significant evidence for condition-dependent splicing within cell type. The two covariance variants should not be added as if they were separate discoveries.

Within each likelihood family, all three approximations had the same empirically calibrated power to the reported precision, and their coefficient bias differed by less than \(2\times10^{-4}\). A second simulation used three isoforms and otherwise retained the same 96 null and 96 effect replicates. Full-covariance and diagonal VI were nearly indistinguishable. Tilted evidence gains had Spearman correlation 0.9999 and median absolute difference 0.073 between posterior families; R\'enyi gains had correlation 0.99996 and median absolute difference 0.044. Every VI variant had calibrated power 0.438 and condition-coefficient RMSE 0.237--0.238. The full-covariance tilted fit took a median 2.43 seconds per nested effect fit versus 2.00 seconds for diagonal tilted VI. Thus, in a controlled multi-isoform setting, omitted posterior covariance was not a material source of error.

The same conclusion held for the direct EC Dirichlet--multinomial. In 96 three-isoform null and effect simulations, diagonal and full ELBO gains had correlation 0.9997 and median absolute difference 0.150; diagonal and full R\'enyi gains had correlation 0.9999 and median difference 0.092. Both posterior families had calibrated power 0.219. In the 65-gene real-data analysis, diagonal and full ELBO gains had correlations 0.998 for condition and 0.9998 for cell type, with median absolute differences 0.0035 and 0.0050. Fixed-draw R\'enyi optimization was again less stable than ELBO optimization.

The genome-wide paired-primer comparison retained 65 genes with mean depth at least 100 EC UMIs per pseudobulk; this was 65 of 66 depth-eligible genes, with one 3,905-EC outlier excluded by the 128-EC computational cap. All retained genes had at most six EC-supported isoforms. The authoritative run initialized each Gaussian variational posterior from the corresponding Laplace means and covariance diagonal, then initialized R\'enyi VI from the ELBO fit. Across the 65 condition models, convergence was 98.5\% for both Laplace likelihoods, 95.4\% for both ELBO variants, 92.3\% for multinomial R\'enyi VI, and 98.5\% for Dirichlet--multinomial R\'enyi VI. Cell-type convergence ranged from 93.8\% to 100\%. There were no fit exceptions.

Approximation agreement depended on isoform dimension. Among the 38 two-isoform genes, multinomial Laplace and R\'enyi evidence gains had Spearman correlation 0.974 for condition and 0.9996 for cell type; Laplace and tilted ELBO correlations were 0.969 and 0.9995. All 16 two-isoform condition genes above the method-specific nominal threshold under multinomial Laplace were also above it under R\'enyi VI. For the 20 three-isoform genes, the analogous gain correlations fell to 0.49 and 0.59. The mean-field posterior has only a diagonal covariance across isoform random effects, whereas Laplace retains their within-mouse posterior covariance. The dimension-dependent divergence therefore motivated a full-covariance extension, but by itself did not distinguish covariance error from objective or optimizer differences. One-dimensional simulation thresholds were not applied to multi-isoform or cell-type omnibus tests.

The tilted surrogate must also be separated from objective evaluation. For two-isoform condition models, median nested gains were 5.54 for the tilted training bound, 4.95 for an exact-likelihood Monte Carlo ELBO evaluated at the same posterior, 4.93 for its R\'enyi evaluation, 4.93 for Laplace, and 4.95 for separately optimized R\'enyi VI. For cell type, the corresponding medians were 89.06, 74.38, 74.36, 72.12, and 74.36. Exact ELBO and R\'enyi gains on the same posterior had Spearman correlations 0.998 and 1.000 for condition and cell type. The tilted bound becomes looser under the larger alternative and therefore overstates nested evidence gains; it is retained as a fitting surrogate, not used as a likelihood-ratio statistic.

Full-covariance results did not support posterior covariance as the cause of the earlier multi-isoform discrepancy. On the real condition analysis, fresh-sample ELBO gains from diagonal and full tilted posteriors had Spearman correlation 0.991 across 65 genes and median absolute difference 0.002. Among the 20 three-isoform genes, the correlation was 0.938 and median difference was 0.024. The corresponding cell-type values were 1.000 overall and within the three-isoform subset, with median three-isoform difference 0.023. These results, together with the three-isoform simulation, point instead to differences in the Laplace objective or its outer optimization. Separately optimizing a fixed-draw full-covariance R\'enyi objective was less stable than fitting the tilted objective and evaluating both exact objectives with fresh draws; the latter is the reported comparison.

The observation-level logistic-normal model was validated with 96 null and 96 effect simulations using three isoforms, true residual standard deviation 0.6, and the same ambiguous paired-primer maps. Tilted full-covariance VI estimated median residual standard deviation 0.559 under the effect and had calibrated power 0.354. Dirichlet--multinomial Laplace had power 0.365, while the misspecified ordinary multinomial had power 0.313. Logistic-normal Laplace had power 0.146 despite estimating residual scale 0.472, indicating that its high-dimensional marginal approximation was not competitive with the tilted fit.

In the real data, tilted logistic-normal fits converged under both nested models for 80.0\% of condition genes and 86.2\% of cell-type genes. The alternative residual standard deviation had median 0.040 for condition and 0.029 for cell type; the respective interquartile ranges were 0.007--0.239 and 0.006--0.234. Relative to the multinomial full posterior, fresh ELBO condition gains changed by a median absolute 0.335 and retained rank correlation 0.809. Cell-type gains changed by 10.68 and retained correlation 0.959, consistent with extra-multinomial variation having more impact on the larger cell-type omnibus statistic. Because residual-scale estimates often approached zero and convergence was weaker, this model is retained as an overdispersion sensitivity analysis rather than replacing the primary test.

\subsection*{Expanded EC likelihood and coverage comparison}

The annotation-level block equivalence relation is insufficient after EC screening: removing unsupported isoforms can make two distinct annotated blocks induce exactly the same partition of the remaining isoforms. The final hypothesis family therefore retains one representative for each tuple of gene, EC-supported isoforms, canonical block-path partition, covered observations, and tested factor levels. This reduces the permissive cell-type family from 5,650 to 4,825 tests and the condition family from 17,685 to 14,833 block-by-cell-type tests over 5,608 representative blocks. This collapse is performed before calibration and BH correction.

The initial empirical calibration compared each test only with its degrees-of-freedom-specific bootstrap pool. After supported-partition deduplication, its minimum attainable p-values were approximately $4.5\times10^{-4}$, whereas BH requires resolution near $10^{-5}$ at the first ranks. Its zero logistic-normal calls were therefore a bootstrap-tail resolution artifact, not disappearance of the cell-type signal.

The former resolved-tail procedure replaced this initial calibration. Its nonredundant deprecated results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:deprecated-primary-ec-results}; the earlier annotation-level counts in Table~\ref{tab:initial-block-results} are retained only to document why supported-partition deduplication and resolved-tail calibration were required.

\subsection*{Cell-type effects within mouse}

The initial Gaussian analysis tested 369 conditional and 218 profile partitions. It found 22 and five FDR-significant partitions, respectively, with all profile discoveries contained in the conditional set. After canonicalizing annotation intervals that encode the same isoform-to-path partition, these 22 intervals represented 14 distinct partitions.

The compositional analysis used converged, conditionally identifiable estimates and required every modeled path proportion to be at least 5\%. This produced 646 nonredundant tests. A minimum of 30 pseudobulk observations per partition, chosen as a label-effect-independent coverage requirement, retained 406 hypotheses for FDR correction. Among these, 149 had exact permutation \(p<0.05\), and \textbf{101 splice-path partitions in 95 genes passed 5\% FDR}. Thirteen of the 14 nonredundant Gaussian discoveries were recovered; the remaining partition had exact \(p=0.032\) but FDR 0.098. Significant compositional results had median maximum cell-type logit effect 0.84 and median effective path count 21.7.

The observation-count threshold is not a narrow boundary effect: thresholds from 29 through 32 pseudobulks each gave 101 discoveries, while 33 gave 99. All 646 observed fits converged. The median number of valid permutations was 500; the minimum was 476 after excluding failed permutation refits. Asymptotic Dirichlet--multinomial p-values were anti-conservative, with 15.5\% of permutation-null values below 0.05, which is why only exact per-event permutation ranks are used for the primary result.

Two sensitivity analyses did not improve the event-level result. Testing 3,708 individual cell-type pairs gave 97 FDR-significant pair hypotheses but only 32 distinct partitions after correction across all pairs. Restricting each block to its two molecule-weighted dominant paths increased coverage; with a 1\% path floor it gave 88 discoveries, and removing the floor gave 91, under a cross-fitted empirical null. These analyses support the full-path exact omnibus test as the primary cell-type analysis.

Allowing the alternative to re-estimate \(\kappa\) also left the result unchanged: it found the same 101 partitions in the same 95 genes, with no event entering or leaving the 5\% FDR set. Exact p-values from fixed- and free-concentration fits had Spearman correlation 0.9995, and both had 148 nominal events. The null optimum was multinomial for 499 of 646 tests, and the alternative optimum was multinomial for 580. Among 66 tests with finite concentration in both models, the median alternative/null concentration ratio was 1.54, consistent with cell-type mean effects explaining some apparent overdispersion. Exact permutation calibration absorbs this nuisance-parameter flexibility, so freeing \(\kappa\) provides no net power gain here.

\subsection*{Calibration}

Across the 25-UMI analysis, 2.7\% of permuted-label Wald p-values were below 0.05 for both conditional and profile covariance, indicating conservative null calibration. A parametric bootstrap of 3,000 reliable local fits gave mean squared standardized error 1.10 and 94.5\% coverage for nominal 95\% intervals. Independent Gaussian simulations of REML--GLS gave type-I error between 2.7\% and 5.0\% across the tested biological-variance settings.

Numerical tests additionally verify skipped-exon block construction, identifiable covariance after collapsing indistinguishable isoforms, rejection of information-null path contrasts, the analytic binomial Fisher variance, and recovery of imposed GLS effects.

\subsection*{Does cell resolution increase condition-test power?}

The cell-resolved likelihood was run on the 527 blocks testable in the 25-UMI primary analysis. All 41,242 block-by-pseudobulk fits converged; 21,624 passed the reliability rules, compared with 21,010 pseudobulk conditional estimates on the same blocks. This produced 836 condition tests, compared with 829 for pseudobulk fitting. No cell-resolved test passed 5\% FDR. Condition-label permutations were conservative, with 2.49\% of null p-values below 0.05.

An exact matched comparison used the same reliable samples for both methods in 807 block-by-cell-type tests. Cell resolution reduced the median local covariance trace by 1.6\%, and the median path-response RMS difference was 0.033 ILR units. P-values were strongly concordant (Spearman rank correlation 0.958); 24 pseudobulk and 25 cell-resolved tests were nominally significant.

For the 259 matched two-condition, two-path tests, mean asymptotic power for a 0.5 ILR effect was 0.504 for both methods. The median ratio of cell-resolved to pseudobulk condition-coefficient standard error was 0.995. Retaining cell likelihood terms therefore did not materially increase power in this dataset. The aggregated EC counts and decoder baseline preserve nearly all relevant local information within a cell type, while residual between-mouse variation dominates the small reduction in inferential variance. Pseudobulk fitting remains the default; the cell-resolved method is available for datasets with stronger within-pseudobulk isoform heterogeneity.

\subsection*{Compositional and joint-test assessment}

Collapsing equivalent isoform partitions reduced the 829 conditional block-by-cell-type fits to 505 nonredundant tests over 317 partitions. The median effective path count was 32 molecules, or 62\% of gene UMIs; only 0.6\% of estimates reached the gene-UMI cap.

On the same 505 tests, conditional GLS had 12 nominal results and the Dirichlet--multinomial had 18. Neither had a 5\% FDR discovery. The Dirichlet--multinomial was conservatively calibrated: 3.76\% of 10,100 permuted-label p-values were below 0.05. In contrast, pure multinomial regression gave 197 nominal and 155 FDR results, but 39.1\% of its permutation p-values were below 0.05. UMI sampling alone therefore cannot represent the large between-mouse compositional variation. Output tables mark pure multinomial and naive shared-effect fits as invalid for inference while retaining them as calibration diagnostics.

ACAT across cell types produced 8 nominal GLS and 12 nominal Dirichlet--multinomial partitions among 317 tests, with no FDR discoveries. Combining distinct partitions within genes gave 7 and 11 nominal genes among 276 tests, again with no FDR discoveries.

Naive shared-effect pooling illustrates the pseudoreplication risk. It gave 43 nominal and one FDR Dirichlet--multinomial partition, but synchronized mouse permutations rejected 13.2\% of nulls at 0.05. The apparent discovery had asymptotic \(p=6.4\times10^{-5}\) but permutation \(p=0.143\). Naive shared GLS was also inflated, with 8.4\% null rejection.

The mouse-clustered shared GLS restored conservative calibration: 2.19\% of 7,169 synchronized permutation p-values were below 0.05. It produced 11 nominal partitions among 359 tests and no FDR discoveries; its smallest p-value was 0.0126 with FDR 0.86. Gene-level ACAT gave 9 nominal genes among 313 and no FDR discoveries. Thus the calibrated gain is limited to the increase from 12 to 18 nominal within-cell-type tests under the Dirichlet--multinomial. None of the evaluated strategies creates a genome-wide condition discovery in this dataset.
