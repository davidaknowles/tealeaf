# Empirical-Bayes path-smoothing strength

This analysis separates selection of the smoothing magnitude from the centering of the downstream penalty. The initial benchmark incorrectly applied both the finite alpha and the baseline-centered empirical-Bayes slab to downstream testing. That made alpha 4--32 incomparable with the uniform-centered alpha sensitivity and caused the apparent power collapse. The corrected alpha-transfer analysis takes only the selected finite scalar and applies it to the existing uniform-centered production likelihood.

For a block with $S_b\in\mathbb N$ paths and pooled label-independent baseline $\bar{\boldsymbol\psi}_b\in\Delta^{S_b-1}$, baseline-centered evidence uses prior counts $\boldsymbol a_b=\alpha S_b\bar{\boldsymbol\psi}_b$ under `per_path` scaling or $\boldsymbol a_b=A\bar{\boldsymbol\psi}_b$ under `total` scaling. The spike is an exact point mass at zero path perturbation. The finite grid is 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, and 128, and the slab normalization includes the $+\tfrac12\log S_b$ ILR-to-simplex Jacobian constant. The spike weight is used only to robustify magnitude selection. Downstream testing uses the selected scalar with uniform centering and independently tests for differential splicing.

`summary.tsv` compares baseline- and uniform-centered evidence, five-fold gene cross-fitting and one global alpha, and both concentration scalings. `selected_strengths.tsv` records the selected values. Baseline evidence selects alpha 4--8 per path or total concentration 8--32. Transferring only these strengths to uniform-centered testing restores the expected gain over alpha 2. The cross-fitted per-path result has 70, 137, and 142 replicated genes on the LeafCutter, MAJIQ Heterogen, and scQuint matched universes, versus 50, 103, and 101 at alpha 2. Cross-fitted fixed-total gives 65, 135, and 139.

Skipping gene-fold cross-validation gives one strength across all genes within each subject half. Baseline evidence selects per-path alpha 4 and 8 in halves 0 and 1, while fixed-total evidence selects $A=16$ in both. The fixed-total global result is the cleanest configuration and gives 68, 139, and 145 replicated genes, held-half replication 0.845, 0.854, and 0.861, and fold rank correlations 0.771, 0.767, and 0.785. Its fold-specific BH counts are 1,442 and 1,017, with pooled null rejection near 0.05 and no fold-level null-family BH calls.

Uniform-centered predictive evidence selects weaker smoothing, per-path alpha 0.5--2 or total concentration 2--4. Cross-fitting does not explain the difference. Uniform EB produces 20/41/45 replicated genes with cross-fitted per-path strength and 40/65/67 with cross-fitted fixed-total strength; the corresponding one-global-alpha values are 19/31/34 and 40/65/62. Held-out count evidence and replicated-discovery power therefore favor different smoothing when the evidence slab itself is uniform centered.

The recommended EB sensitivity is baseline-centered spike-and-slab evidence, alpha-only transfer to uniform-centered testing, fixed total concentration, and one global alpha across genes. In these subject splits it selects $A=16$. This remains internally evaluated on the benchmark splits and needs external confirmation before replacing the reported alpha-2 production result.
