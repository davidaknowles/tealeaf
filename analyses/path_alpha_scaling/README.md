# Path-smoothing concentration scaling

`summary.tsv` compares a global fixed total Dirichlet concentration (A) with the previous global per-path pseudocount (alpha_{m path}) in the calibrated pairwise subject-split benchmark. Rows match the two parameterizations at (A=2alpha_{m path}), so their prior counts are identical for two-path blocks. Two-path blocks comprise 5,295 of 7,671 fold-0 candidates, or 69.0%. For a block with (S_b>2) paths, the previous rule has total concentration (S_balpha_{m path}=S_bA/2), whereas the fixed-total rule retains concentration (A).

Both parameterizations use uniform centering, robust variance moderation, leave-one-block-out empirical calibration, 32 sign-flip null families per fold, Simes gene aggregation, two-fold conjunction, and BH at 0.05. `total_*` columns describe fixed-total fits and `per_path_*` columns describe the saved current-scaling fits. Replicated-gene and held-half columns appear in LeafCutter, MAJIQ Heterogen, and scQuint order.

Both curves reach maximum replicated-gene counts of 78, 161, and 161 on the three comparator-matched universes. Fixed-total (A=64) gives 76, 161, and 161, compared with 78, 161, and 161 under matched current scaling (alpha_{m path}=32). Fixed-total (A=128) gives 78, 156, and 157 and has held-half replication 0.909, 0.919, and 0.906, nearly identical to 0.908, 0.919, and 0.907 under matched current scaling. Pooled null rejection remains approximately 0.05 and no fold-level null family makes a BH call at any fixed-total setting. Fixed total concentration is therefore the preferable dimension-invariant parameterization, with no material loss at the observed optimum. The headline and full-data results retain the prior per-path production fit because this comparison is post-hoc on the same subject splits.
